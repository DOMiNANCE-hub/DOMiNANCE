## Worked description of a small dataset
Enter the fictional observations 2, 4 and 9 in A2:A4. Before using functions, arrange them in order and calculate their total: 15. Divide by three observations to get a mean of 5. The middle ordered value is 4, the median. The difference between mean and median is informative: the larger observation affects the arithmetic mean without becoming the middle observation.

| Question | Function or reasoning | Result |
|---|---|---:|
| How much altogether? | SUM(A2:A4) | 15 |
| What is the arithmetic average? | AVERAGE(A2:A4) | 5 |
| What is the middle ordered value? | MEDIAN(A2:A4) | 4 |
| How many numeric observations? | COUNT(A2:A4) | 3 |
| What is the largest observation? | MAX(A2:A4) | 9 |

Choosing a function follows the scientific question. A mean is not a total merely because both use all observations. A mode requires attention to repetition: replacing 4 with 2 gives 2, 2, 9, whose most frequent value is 2.

## Missing is different from zero
In B2:B5 enter 10, 12, pending and leave the final cell genuinely empty. COUNT gives 2; COUNTA gives 3. The pending label signals a nonnumeric entry; it should not silently become a measured zero. A cell containing a space also differs from an empty cell, which helps explain why replacing unwanted values with spaces is unreliable.
For counts 35, 40, 55, 40, test >=40 by evaluating every row: false, true, true, true. The count is 3. Changing the criterion to >40 changes the two boundary outcomes and leaves only 55. This is operator behavior, not an institution's pass/fail policy.

## Interpret spread before selecting a formula
Ask whether the listed observations are the entire population of interest or a sample used to estimate a wider population. The lecturer's STDEV/STDEVP distinction follows that choice. A sample of a large population does not become “the population” simply because every value available in the worksheet was selected.
Check: if all measurements increased by the same amount, would that necessarily make them more spread out relative to one another? No: their separations would be unchanged. This distinguishes the location of measurements from their variability without assigning an unreviewed statistical formula.

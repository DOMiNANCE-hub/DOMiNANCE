## Build a cross-sheet dependency
Create a source sheet named CSM 1 and enter 7 in A10. On another worksheet enter ='CSM 1'!A10. Read the expression in parts: quoted sheet name, exclamation mark, then cell address. Its predicted result is the source value 7. Change the source to 9 and inspect the dependent formula.
Single quotes preserve the sheet name containing a space. The exclamation mark separates sheet identity from cell coordinates; a colon instead denotes a range. Moving the exclamation mark after the address reverses the reference's structure, not just its appearance.

## A four-sheet worked total
Create four consecutively arranged worksheets, Sheet1 through Sheet4. Put the values 1 through 10 in D1:D10 of each. First calculate a single-sheet total: 1+2+...+10=55. Then use =SUM(Sheet1:Sheet4!D1:D10) in a separate summary location. The intended four-sheet total is 4*55=220, matching the independently executed alternative-engine arithmetic check.

| Expression part | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Sheet1:Sheet4 | Inclusive worksheet span in tab order |
| ! | Boundary between sheets and cell range |
| D1:D10 | Inclusive cell range within each included sheet |
| SUM(...) | Aggregation of the referenced values |

The two colons answer different questions: which worksheets and which cells. The worksheet span is not restricted to just its two endpoints. Inspect tab order rather than treating names alone as a guarantee that only four sheets lie between the endpoints.

## Diagnose a surprising total
If the result is 110, check whether only two sheets were selected. If an extra intervening source sheet contributes data, investigate the span before changing the arithmetic function. A correct SUM of the wrong worksheets remains an incorrect summary for the stated exercise.
Absolute cell references such as $D$1:$D$10 concern the coordinates when a formula is copied. They do not redefine the included worksheet span. A cross-sheet reference is not automatically circular; a circular dependency exists only if the calculation ultimately depends on its own output.

## Two original chart cases
For a department comparison, enter Department and Count as headings, followed by fictional counts A=12, B=18, C=9. Select the labels and counts together, insert a column chart through the chart tools available in your version, and inspect the plotted heights. B should be highest. The department names are categories, not measured numeric x coordinates.
For a time-spacing example, use paired observations:

| Day | Count |
|---|---:|
| 1 | 10 |
| 3 | 16 |
| 10 | 20 |

Use an XY scatter chart when numeric spacing matters. The horizontal gap from day 3 to day 10 should represent seven days, compared with two days from 1 to 3. Treating the three labels as equally spaced categories would hide that difference. A plausible rising line is not enough to establish correct axis meaning.

## Inspect what the chart actually contains
After insertion, trace a visible point or column back to its worksheet row. Check the series name against the heading, the category labels against the intended groups, and the axes against the units. The title is an explanation you provide, not a validation that the selected cells match it.
Change the fictional B count from 18 to 8 and inspect a source-linked chart. The relative heights should change accordingly. If they do not, inspect the source selection and series rather than changing the chart border. If the plotted values look unreasonable, examine the data before choosing a more decorative chart style.

## Parts of a whole require a meaningful whole
A pie chart can show the three department counts as parts of their combined count, provided that interpretation makes sense for the dataset. It cannot faithfully represent the relationship between observation day and count as two numeric variables. Multiple unrelated series do not become one coherent whole just because the software offers slices.
Check: could a chart be visually polished and still misleading? Yes, through the wrong range, labels, units or axis interpretation. The appropriate repair depends on the reason for the mismatch, not merely on appearance.

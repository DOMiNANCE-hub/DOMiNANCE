# Cross-sheet and three-dimensional references

## Learning goals
Read a sheet-qualified reference and distinguish a sheet span from a cell range.

## One other worksheet
An exclamation mark separates a sheet name from a cell address. =Sheet5!A10 reads A10 from Sheet5. When a sheet name contains a space, single quotes surround the sheet name: ='CSM 1'!A10. The quotes belong around the whole name, not around the cell address.
A cross-sheet reference does not itself mean a circular dependency. Circularity concerns a formula depending on its own result, directly or through other cells.

## The same cells across multiple worksheets
The lecturer's =SUM(Sheet1:Sheet4!$D$1:$D$10) totals D1:D10 across the worksheet span from Sheet1 through Sheet4, inclusive. The colon between sheet names selects a sheet span. The colon between D1 and D10 selects a cell range. These are distinct parts of the expression.
Dollar signs fix the cell coordinates when copying a formula; they do not turn the sheet span into a set of columns. Worksheet order matters to what lies between the endpoint tabs. Inspect the source sheets before relying on a multi-sheet total.

## Apply it
On a fictional four-sheet workbook, place the values 1 through 10 in D1:D10 on each sheet. Each sheet totals 55; the four-sheet total is 220. Verify the formula in the local calculation engine before interpreting it. This created example is independent arithmetic, not an official practical assessment.

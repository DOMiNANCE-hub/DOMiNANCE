# Editing, AutoComplete and AutoFill

## Learning goals
Choose between clearing and deleting, recognise AutoComplete, and use AutoFill without assuming an unintended pattern.

## Clearing changes contents; deletion changes the grid
Clear Contents removes the value or formula while leaving the cell and its formatting in place. Deleting cells removes them and shifts surrounding cells according to the chosen direction. Deleting an entire row removes that row. Before deleting, consider whether other entries must move together.

## Two different time savers
AutoComplete suggests an existing text entry from the same column as you type its opening characters. Accept the suggestion with Enter or continue typing a different entry. It is useful for repeated labels; it is not a general method for generating numeric sequences.
AutoFill uses the fill handle at a selection's edge to copy data, extend a detected series or fill formulas into adjacent cells. Selecting 2 and 4 as the first two entries establishes a difference of 2; extending that series produces 6, 8 and 10. A single number alone may be copied, so inspect the result and the Auto Fill Options rather than assuming that Excel knows your intention.

## Copying formulas is not copying their displayed answers
When a formula is filled, relative references adjust to its new position. A repeated formula may therefore produce different results on different rows. The next lesson explains when a reference should adjust and when it should remain fixed.
Ctrl+D fills down from the entry above; Ctrl+R fills right from the entry to the left. Both can overwrite existing values. Check the destination range first and use Undo if the fill is wrong.

## Try it
Enter Monday and Tuesday, then extend a short series. Repeat with 2 and 4. Finally copy a text label. Describe why the series and copied label behave differently.

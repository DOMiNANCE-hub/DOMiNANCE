# Spreadsheets and dependable data entry

## Learning goals
Explain what a spreadsheet does, distinguish text from numbers, and edit a value deliberately.

## From a worksheet to a calculation
A spreadsheet arranges data in rows and columns. Their intersection is a cell. A worksheet is a sheet of cells; a workbook holds worksheets. Use a spreadsheet to store, compare and calculate data, then communicate results with charts. The lecturer notes describe financial planning, scientific calculations and hospital data as applications. Software performs the calculation you specify; you still need to check the inputs and their meaning.

## Enter the right kind of value
Enter quantities as numbers when you intend to calculate with them. Text labels describe those quantities. A fictional equipment register might have an item name in A2, quantity 4 in B2 and unit price 12 in C2. Keep units in a heading such as “Price (GHS)” rather than typing “12 cedis” into each numeric cell.
An identifier is different from a quantity. Enter an apostrophe before a numeric-looking identifier, such as '0017, to preserve it as text. The apostrophe marks the entry as text rather than becoming part of the displayed identifier. Do not assume that everything made of digits should be added.

## Edit and check
Select a cell to inspect its contents in the formula bar. Double-click a cell to edit it; Enter accepts the change and Escape cancels the edit. Clear an unwanted entry with Delete or Clear Contents rather than replacing it with a space. Clearing contents leaves the cell in place, whereas deleting a cell can move other cells.

## Try it
Create three fictional equipment rows with numeric quantities and prices. Correct one quantity, cancel a second edit, then clear one entry. Explain why the item identifier should stay text. This is a practice exercise, not real patient data.

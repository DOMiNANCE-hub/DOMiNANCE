# Sigma and pi bonds, hybridisation and restricted rotation

## Learning goals
Count sigma and pi bonds in simple molecules. Relate sp, sp² and sp³ models to local geometry and explain why a pi bond constrains molecular shape.

## Overlap and local bonding models
A sigma bond has electron density along the internuclear axis in the simple orbital-overlap description. A pi bond involves lateral overlap with density on opposite sides of that axis and a nodal plane containing the axis. In ordinary introductory carbon structures, a single bond is sigma, a double bond contains one sigma plus one pi, and a triple bond contains one sigma plus two pi bonds.

Hybridisation is a model that combines atomic-orbital functions to describe directional local bonding. Four sp³ orbitals point approximately tetrahedrally, three sp² orbitals lie in a trigonal plane with one unhybridised p orbital left over, and two sp orbitals point linearly with two unhybridised p orbitals. Hybrid orbitals point in different directions; “equal direction” in the source definition must not be read as all pointing the same way.

For a carbon with four sigma bonds, an sp³ description is often useful. Around each alkene carbon, an sp² sigma framework leaves a p orbital available for the pi bond. Around an alkyne carbon, sp sigma bonding leaves two p orbitals for two perpendicular pi interactions. These local assignments do not mean a carbon atom must physically undergo an independently observable promotion step before every bond forms. The energy of the bonded system is what matters.

## Worked bond counts
Ethane, CH₃–CH₃, has six C–H sigma bonds and one C–C sigma bond: seven sigma and zero pi. Ethene, CH₂=CH₂, has four C–H sigma bonds plus one C–C sigma bond and one pi bond: five sigma and one pi. Ethyne, HC≡CH, has two C–H sigma bonds, one C–C sigma bond and two pi bonds: three sigma and two pi.

Count one sigma connection for each bonded atom pair, then add the pi components of multiple bonds. Calling the entire C=C connection “one pi bond” loses its sigma component. Counting two sigmas for a double bond gives the wrong overlap picture.

## Why rotation matters
Rotation about a C=C bond would disrupt the alignment needed for pi overlap. This produces a substantial barrier and permits configurational E/Z distinctions when each carbon has two different substituents. Pi bonding therefore helps determine molecular shape: the lecturer's statement that it has no role in shape is not adopted.

Many sigma bonds permit conformational rotation, but “free rotation” is an approximation. Torsional and steric energy barriers remain, and conjugation or ring constraints can limit rotation substantially. A double bond is stronger overall than the corresponding single bond in a simple comparison, even though its pi component may be more accessible in particular reactions. “Pi weaker than sigma” does not mean the entire double bond is weaker than a single bond or that every pi-containing molecule reacts identically.

## Short concept checks
- How many sigma and pi bonds occur in ethene? **Answer:** Five sigma and one pi.
- Why can an alkene retain distinct E and Z configurations? **Answer:** Rotation would disrupt pi overlap, so it is restricted; each alkene carbon must also have two different substituents.

## Key takeaways
Separate whole bond order from sigma/pi components. Hybridisation describes a local bonding model, while overlap and energetic constraints explain the geometry and limits of rotation.

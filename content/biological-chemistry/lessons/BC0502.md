# Fischer projections, D/L and sugar stereoisomers

## Learning goals
Read a Fischer projection, identify the D/L reference centre and distinguish enantiomers, diastereomers and epimers. Work out stereoisomer counts without conflating configuration with optical rotation.

## How to read a sugar Fischer projection
Place the open-chain carbon backbone vertically with the most oxidised end near the top. Number from the end that gives the carbonyl its appropriate low position: glucose has aldehyde C1, while fructose has ketone C2. At a Fischer cross, horizontal bonds project toward the observer and vertical bonds away. The drawing is a projection convention, not a flat molecule.

For ordinary aldoses and ketoses in this orientation, examine the stereocentre furthest from the carbonyl. An OH on its right gives the D family; on its left gives L. For an aldohexose this reference is C5, not C1 and not whichever OH happens to be nearest the top of the page. D does not mean that every OH points right, and it does not establish a (+) measured rotation. D-fructose illustrates why family and optical sign must be kept separate.

## Worked glucose, mannose and galactose comparison
In the conventional D-glucose Fischer projection, OH positions at C2,C3,C4,C5 are right,left,right,right. D-mannose has left,left,right,right: only C2 changes, so glucose and mannose are C2 epimers. D-galactose has right,left,left,right: only C4 differs from glucose, so they are C4 epimers. Both comparisons retain D because the C5 reference relationship is unchanged.

Glucose and galactose are diastereomers, not mirror images. D-glucose and L-glucose invert all corresponding stereocentres and are enantiomers. Mannose versus galactose differ at C2 and C4, so they are diastereomers but not a one-centre epimer pair. An epimer differs at exactly one stereocentre; it is not a synonym for every isomer.

## Worked counting and projection mistakes
An open-chain aldohexose has four conventional stereocentres, C2–C5, so its unconstrained maximum is 2⁴=16 configurations, organised into eight D/L pairs. An ordinary open-chain 2-ketohexose has three, C3–C5, giving up to 2³=8. These counts concern the stated open-chain structures; cyclisation creates an anomeric centre and should not be silently included in the same count.

Count actual stereocentres, not OH groups or carbon atoms. The carbonyl carbon is planar rather than a conventional tetrahedral stereocentre; a terminal CH₂OH has two identical hydrogens. Dihydroxyacetone has no stereocentre and therefore no D/L pair of the usual kind. In general, 2ⁿ is a maximum before symmetry, not proof that every compound with n centres has exactly that many distinct stereoisomers.

## Short concept checks
- Glucose and galactose differ at C4 while both have the C5 OH on the right. Are they D/L enantiomers? **Answer:** No. They are D-family C4 epimers and therefore diastereomers.
- Why is assigning D from glucose's C2 OH unreliable? **Answer:** D/L is determined by the reference stereocentre furthest from the carbonyl, C5 in the conventional aldohexose projection.

## Key takeaways
Establish projection orientation before assigning configuration. Identify the differing centres explicitly; D/L, R/S, anomeric α/β and measured rotation answer different questions.

# Important monosaccharides and biological applications

## Learning goals
Compare glucose, fructose, galactose, ribose and deoxyribose. Explain biological examples while distinguishing a biochemical defect from unsupported universal clinical claims.

## Three familiar hexoses
Glucose is an aldohexose used in energy metabolism and biosynthesis. Dextrose is a familiar name for D-glucose. Fructose is a ketohexose with its open-chain carbonyl at C2; it is a constitutional isomer of glucose rather than a glucose epimer. Galactose is an aldohexose and glucose's C4 epimer. Galactose is a component of lactose; lactose is the principal milk carbohydrate, so the slide's phrase about galactose as the principal simple milk sugar should not be read as an assertion that milk carbohydrate is predominantly free galactose.

Insulin and glucagon participate in blood-glucose regulation, but their names do not define a diagnostic cutoff. The slide combines a glucose range with a post-meal statement without specifying sampling conditions. Fasting, post-meal, whole-blood/plasma and clinical context differ. No universal normal range or diagnosis is scored from that unqualified slide wording.

## Lactose hydrolysis and galactose handling are different
Lactase in the intestine hydrolyses lactose to glucose and galactose. Low lactase activity can leave lactose unabsorbed, contributing to osmotic effects and bacterial fermentation. Galactosaemia instead concerns defects in galactose metabolism after the sugar is available. In classic galactosaemia, deficient GALT activity impairs processing of galactose 1-phosphate; accumulated metabolites can contribute to severe illness. Other enzyme defects produce other galactosemia forms, so one enzyme should not be assigned to every form indiscriminately.

Early dietary management reduces severe acute complications in classic disease, but long-term complications can still occur despite early appropriate restriction. The lecturer's statement that all effects are entirely avoided by a galactose-free diet is therefore not adopted. This comparison teaches pathway location and substrate accumulation; it is not individual dietary or treatment advice, and exact clinical management requires qualified assessment.

## Five-carbon sugars in information chemistry
Ribose contributes to RNA and many nucleotide/coenzyme structures; 2-deoxyribose contributes to DNA. Both retain five carbons, but the C2 substitution differs. Free sugars can adopt multiple forms in solution, while a sugar bound in a nucleoside has a specific linkage and configuration. Do not infer that a free-sugar drawing alone supplies every feature of the nucleotide.

Sugar derivatives also contribute to cell-surface recognition, including blood-group glycans. Recognition depends on specific terminal residues and linkages rather than the presence of any carbohydrate. Detailed blood-group structures and diagnostic interpretation are not inferred from the brief source statements alone.

## Short concept checks
- Why are glucose and fructose not a C2 epimer pair? **Answer:** Their open-chain carbonyl connectivity differs (aldose versus ketose); an epimer comparison requires the same connectivity and one differing stereocentre.
- Does inability to hydrolyse lactose identify classic GALT deficiency? **Answer:** No. Lactase deficiency concerns intestinal disaccharide cleavage; classic galactosaemia concerns downstream galactose metabolism.

## Key takeaways
Relate each molecule to its actual structure and pathway. Source ranges and disease simplifications require context before becoming clinical teaching or assessment.

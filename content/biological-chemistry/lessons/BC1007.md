# Glycerophospholipid backbones, head groups and charge

## Learning goals
Identify the common glycerophospholipid architecture, compare head-group chemistry and distinguish phospholipid phosphate groups from the neutral storage structure of a triacylglycerol.

## Two acyl chains and a phosphate-bearing region
A common diacyl glycerophospholipid has fatty-acyl esters at the sn-1 and sn-2 positions of glycerol and a phosphate-containing group at sn-3. The sn labels specify a stereochemical numbering convention; they are not the number of double bonds. Phosphatidic acid is a central simple member/intermediate, while attachment of an additional polar alcohol-derived head group produces other classes.

Tail composition and head identity both matter. Replacing one fatty-acyl chain can alter packing without changing a phosphatidylcholine head group. Changing the head group can alter charge and recognition even if both tails remain identical. A membrane therefore contains molecular species within each class, not just one universal phosphatidylcholine molecule.

## Head groups contribute specific chemistry
Phosphatidylcholine, PC, contains a quaternary-ammonium choline group. Phosphatidylethanolamine, PE, commonly contains protonated ethanolamine. Their positive groups can balance the negative phosphodiester charge under a simple near-neutral model, giving zwitterionic molecules with approximately zero net charge. Zero net charge does not mean no charged groups or no interaction with water.

Phosphatidylserine, PS, adds a carboxylate as well as an amino group; in the ordinary near-neutral model its net charge is negative. Phosphatidylglycerol, PG, and phosphatidylinositol, PI, also commonly contribute negative charge. Additional phosphorylation of an inositol ring creates distinct phosphoinositides with different charges and signalling roles; unmodified PI and every phosphorylated derivative should not be assigned one identical formula or function.

Phosphatidic-acid ionisation and apparent charge can vary with environment because a phosphate monoester differs from a phosphodiester. Avoid inferring a universal exact charge at every pH solely from the word phosphate. State the protonation model when calculating a head-group net charge.

## Cardiolipin provides a four-tail example
Cardiolipin is often called diphosphatidylglycerol. A useful connectivity description is two phosphatidic-acid-like units linked through a central glycerol, giving four acyl chains and two phosphate groups in the common structure. Count the actual backbone/links rather than treating it as a two-tailed ordinary PC with a longer name.

It is important in the inner mitochondrial membrane and also occurs in bacteria, so discovery in heart tissue does not make it exclusive to heart or to mitochondria. Its multi-tail architecture and interactions support specialised membrane organisation. No universal percentage or exact diagnostic concentration follows from that role.

## Worked charge comparison
Under an explicitly supplied model, assign one phosphodiester −1, a choline quaternary ammonium +1, a serine ammonium +1 and a serine carboxylate −1. PC then sums to −1+1=0. PS sums to −1+1−1=−1. The difference arises from the extra carboxylate, not from PS suddenly lacking all polar groups.

This is a formal-charge accounting example for common states, not a calculation of every membrane's surface potential. Ion binding, pH, lipid distribution and protein partners also affect physical behaviour. An outer leaflet and inner leaflet need not have identical composition or exposure of the same head groups.

## Short concept checks
- How many fatty-acyl tails occur in the common cardiolipin structure? **Answer:** Four, associated with its two phosphatidic-acid-like units and central glycerol organisation.
- Why can PC be zwitterionic while PS is net negative in the supplied model? **Answer:** Both have opposing phosphate/ammonium charges, but PS also contributes a carboxylate.

## Key takeaways
Identify tail links, phosphate chemistry and head group separately. Formal charge, membrane location and biological function are related but distinct claims.

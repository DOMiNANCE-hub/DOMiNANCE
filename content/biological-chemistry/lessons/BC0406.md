# Molecular forces, hydrogen bonds and dissolution

## Learning goals
Compare dispersion, dipole interactions, hydrogen bonding and ion–dipole interactions. Explain solubility and phase behaviour without replacing a full energetic argument with “like dissolves like”.

## Several interactions can coexist
London dispersion arises from correlated fluctuations in electron distributions. It occurs in polar as well as nonpolar species. Greater polarizability and effective contact can strengthen these interactions; molecular size and shape both matter. The gas/liquid/solid comparison among halogens illustrates a broad trend, but temperature and pressure must be stated when assigning phases.

Polar molecules can also experience permanent dipole–dipole interactions. Hydrogen bonding is a directional interaction involving a hydrogen attached to a suitable electronegative donor and an appropriate electron-rich acceptor. In common biological examples, O–H or N–H groups donate and suitable oxygen or nitrogen lone pairs accept. Not every nitrogen or oxygen is an equivalent acceptor: protonation and resonance can make a major difference. Hydrogen bonding can occur between molecules or within one molecule.

Ions interacting with polar water molecules provide ion–dipole interactions. These are not simply hydrogen bonds between Na+ and water. Water orients its partial-negative oxygen region toward cations and its partial-positive hydrogen regions toward anions, while the full hydration structure remains dynamic.

## Worked boiling and solubility comparisons
Compare an alcohol with an ether of similar size. An alcohol has an O–H group and can donate and accept hydrogen bonds in common neutral examples. An ether oxygen can accept hydrogen bonds from water, but the ether lacks O–H and cannot donate in the same way. Ethers are not universally nonpolar or unable to interact with water; molecular size and the hydrocarbon portion affect overall solubility.

For NaCl dissolution, energy is required to separate ions from their solid and reorganise water; hydration contributes favourable interactions, and entropy also contributes to the free-energy change. ΔG = ΔH − TΔS summarises why comparing only one pairwise attraction is insufficient. Some ionic solids remain sparingly soluble. No rule here says every ionic compound dissolves in water.

Wax and water do interact through forces, but mixing is unfavourable under ordinary conditions because the total free-energy balance differs from that for more compatible nonpolar solvents. The hydrophobic effect is not proof of zero attraction, nor is it an additional covalent bond joining hydrocarbon groups.

## Biological consequences and source qualifications
Hydrogen bonding, dispersion, charge interactions and hydration help stabilise protein and nucleic-acid structures. DNA strand association also involves base stacking and solvent effects; it is not explained solely by counting hydrogen bonds. Such interactions can influence recognition and reaction energetics, so saying intermolecular forces affect only physical properties and never chemical behaviour is too absolute.

HF has an unusually high boiling point relative to the other hydrogen halides, but “HF is liquid” requires stated temperature and pressure; its normal boiling point is near 20°C. The intended comparison concerns strong association, not a universal phase label. Unsupported blanket claims about HCl hydrogen bonding, nonpolar ether or all salts dissolving are not adopted as assessment keys.

## Short concept checks
- Can a neutral ether accept a hydrogen bond from water despite lacking an O–H bond? **Answer:** Yes. Its oxygen can act as an acceptor; absence of O–H primarily limits donation.
- Why does a strong hydration interaction not alone prove high solubility? **Answer:** Lattice separation, solvent reorganisation and entropy also contribute to the total free-energy balance.

## Key takeaways
Name the interacting species and recognise simultaneous forces. Use phase conditions and a complete thermodynamic comparison; slogans and one-interaction explanations have important limits.

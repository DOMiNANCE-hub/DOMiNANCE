# Dietary amino acids, modified residues and biological derivatives

## Learning goals
Distinguish dietary indispensability from side-chain classification, explain representative residue modifications and separate protein-incorporated residues from derived signalling molecules.

## Essential depends on organism and supply
An indispensable or essential amino acid must be supplied because endogenous synthesis cannot meet requirements. This classification differs from “basic”, “polar” or “aromatic”, which describe chemistry. In adult humans, the nine routinely classified indispensable amino acids are histidine, isoleucine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, threonine, tryptophan and valine. Histidine belongs in this list; the lecturer table's “semi-essential” label is not adopted as a general human rule.

Conditionally indispensable means synthesis may be insufficient in particular developmental or physiological circumstances. Arginine is an important example. Nonessential does not mean unimportant or that no intake can contribute: it refers to synthetic capacity relative to need. Species and life stage matter. A missing carbon-skeleton biosynthetic pathway cannot be supplied merely by assuming that transamination creates every amino acid from nothing. Transamination transfers an amino group between an amino acid and a suitable keto acid; the necessary skeleton and enzymes must exist.

## Complementarity without dietary absolutes
Protein synthesis requires an adequate available mixture of amino acids as well as energy and other nutrients. If one indispensable amino acid is limiting, abundant amounts of others do not directly substitute for it in a specified protein sequence. Cereals are often relatively limited in lysine, while many legumes are relatively limited in sulfur amino acids. Combining foods can improve the pattern, but species, processing, digestibility and total intake affect the result. These are trends, not universal numerical compositions for every grain and bean.

The slides link prolonged protein deficiency with kwashiorkor. Severe malnutrition has interacting dietary, infectious and metabolic factors; neither one low amino-acid value nor oedema alone establishes a diagnosis. This lesson teaches biochemical requirements, not a treatment prescription or a rigid disease-by-one-residue rule.

## Modifications extend the encoded chemistry
A ribosomally inserted residue may be modified after incorporation. Collagen contains hydroxyproline and hydroxylysine generated from appropriate proline and lysine residues. Hydroxylation supports collagen maturation; vitamin C supports the relevant hydroxylase chemistry. That is different from interpreting each hydroxy residue as a separate ordinary codon assignment.

Reversible phosphorylation can add phosphate to Ser, Thr or Tyr hydroxyl groups, changing charge and recognition. Lysine acetylation neutralises the positive charge of its ε-amino group under ordinary biological conditions; methylation has different chemical consequences and should not be treated as an interchangeable modification. γ-Carboxylation converts selected glutamate residues into calcium-binding γ-carboxyglutamate in several clotting-related proteins. Not every protein undergoes every modification, and phosphorylation does not universally switch all enzymes “on”.

## Derivatives need not be protein residues
GABA is formed by decarboxylating glutamate; histamine derives from histidine decarboxylation. Removing CO2 changes structure and function. Serotonin is derived from tryptophan through more than one step; adrenaline derives from tyrosine. The precursor relationship does not make each product a standard residue inserted into ordinary proteins.

Thyroid hormone formation involves iodination and coupling of selected tyrosine-derived groups in thyroglobulin, followed by proteolytic release. Thus, released T3/T4 are not themselves intact protein hormones. β-Alanine occurs in biologically important small molecules including carnosine and the pantothenate component of coenzyme A, while differing from an α-amino acid in the position of its amino group. Rare or insufficiently resolved source claims are retained in the review ledger rather than made into scored facts.

## Short concept checks
- Is “nonessential” equivalent to “has no biological role”? **Answer:** No. It means endogenous synthesis can normally meet needs in the specified context.
- Does phosphorylation create a new underlying amino-acid sequence? **Answer:** It modifies selected residues after incorporation; the encoded sequence and chemical modification should be described separately.

## Key takeaways
Dietary categories describe supply; chemical categories describe groups. Distinguish ordinary encoded residues, post-translational modifications and metabolites derived from amino acids.

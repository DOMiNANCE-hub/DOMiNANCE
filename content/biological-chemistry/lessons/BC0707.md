# Peptide counts, sequence information and hydrolysis limits

## Learning goals
Calculate backbone bonds, water balances and simple sequence spaces. Distinguish composition from sequence and interpret amino-acid analysis with its chemical limitations.

## Count connections before choosing a formula
For a single ordinary linear peptide with n residues, there are n−1 peptide bonds. Formally assembling it from n free amino acids releases n−1 water molecules. Complete hydrolysis reverses that balance. This applies to the specified linear backbone, not every cyclic or cross-linked molecule. A cyclic chain with every end joined has different counting and no ordinary free termini.

For c separate unmodified linear chains with N total residues, the total backbone peptide count is N−c. A disulfide connecting two chains does not create another backbone peptide bond. In a hypothetical two-chain protein with chain lengths 21 and 30, there are (21−1)+(30−1)=49 peptide bonds, 51 residues and two N plus two C termini before terminal modifications. Counting 50 bonds would incorrectly treat the two chains as one continuous backbone.

## Worked mass and combinatorial examples
If supplied free amino-acid molar masses are glycine 75, alanine 89 and serine 105 g/mol, the formal mass of the unmodified linear Gly–Ala–Ser peptide is 75+89+105−2×18=233 g/mol using the supplied water mass of 18 g/mol. These rounded teaching values are explicit; do not add them to an already residue-based mass table and subtract water again.

For an oriented four-residue chain with twenty independently permitted residue choices at each position, there are 20^4=160,000 possible sequences. This assumes repetition is allowed, no composition restriction and no imposed biological selection. If instead the chain contains exactly one each of four distinct specified residues, its possible orders number 4!=24. If two glycines and one alanine are specified, the three distinguishable orders are GGA, GAG and AGG: 3!/2!=3. State what is held fixed before applying a combinatorial formula.

## Composition does not establish order
Amino-acid composition counts residue types; sequence tells their order from N to C. Complete hydrolysis of Gly–Ala–Ser and Ser–Gly–Ala produces the same ideal composition. It therefore cannot, alone, determine which sequence was present. Sequencing or suitable overlapping fragments supply different evidence. A protein's primary sequence is encoded through gene expression, but mature chains can undergo cleavage and chemical modification, so the final molecule may require more description than a raw translated string.

## What acid analysis loses
The lecturer describes strong-acid hydrolysis, commonly using 6 M HCl and heating near 110°C for analytical work. These are source conditions, not a student laboratory protocol or a procedure executed here. Tryptophan is destroyed under ordinary acid-hydrolysis conditions; serine and threonine can be progressively lost; some valine/isoleucine-containing linkages hydrolyse slowly. Time-course interpretation and separate methods may be needed. “No racemisation under every acid condition” is not a defensible absolute.

Asparagine and glutamine side-chain amides can be hydrolysed to aspartate and glutamate, with ammonia release. Consequently, the measured combined categories are Asx=Asp+Asn and Glx=Glu+Gln, rather than a unique original count of each. A source sentence naming “Asp and Gln” alongside the paired categories is clarified by the actual chemistry. Ammonia alone cannot give a complete original sequence or uniquely separate all sources of nitrogen. Cysteine and methionine also require appropriate analytical handling for reliable recovery.

Suppose idealised analysis reports ten Asx residues. It does not tell whether these were six Asp plus four Asn or another mixture summing to ten. Nor does a missing tryptophan peak prove the original protein contained no tryptophan. Absence of an analyte after destructive treatment differs from evidence of absence in the starting protein.

## Short concept checks
- How many backbone peptide bonds are in separate unmodified 21- and 30-residue chains? **Answer:** 20+29=49; an interchain disulfide does not add a peptide bond.
- Can ten Asx residues uniquely establish an original Asp count? **Answer:** No. The combined acid-analysis category includes residues originally present as Asp or Asn.

## Key takeaways
Identify chain topology, mass convention and sequence constraints. Analytical destruction can erase distinctions; never infer sequence or original absence from composition alone.

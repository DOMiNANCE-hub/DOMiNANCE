# Building electronic configurations

## Learning goals
Use Aufbau, Pauli and Hund together. Translate between shell totals, subshell notation and orbital occupancy without confusing capacity with actual filling order.

## Three rules answer three questions
An electronic configuration states how electrons occupy available orbitals. First determine the electron count: a neutral atom has Z electrons, while an ion's charge changes that count. Then allocate those electrons with the appropriate energy order and occupancy rules.

The Aufbau approach fills lower-energy available states before higher ones as a useful ground-state construction scheme. A common sequence through the first transition series is 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p. The numerical shell label alone therefore does not determine the entire filling order. This scheme has known exceptions; it is a starting method rather than an exact independent law for every atom and ion.

Pauli's exclusion principle states that no two electrons in one atom have the same four quantum numbers. Two electrons sharing an orbital already share n, ℓ and mℓ, so they must have opposite ms. An orbital may be empty, singly occupied or doubly occupied, but cannot contain three electrons.

Hund's rule concerns degenerate orbitals: electrons occupy them singly with parallel spins before pairing in the ground-state arrangement. For 2p³, place one electron in each of three p orbitals. For 2p⁴, the fourth electron pairs in one orbital while two remain singly occupied. Hund does not instruct an electron to enter a higher-energy subshell simply to avoid all pairing anywhere.

## Worked nitrogen, oxygen and sodium
Nitrogen has seven electrons: 1s² 2s² 2p³. The first four fill 1s and 2s; the remaining three occupy separate p orbitals. In an orbital-box shorthand, 2p is `[↑] [↑] [↑]`. Oxygen has eight electrons and configuration 1s² 2s² 2p⁴, with one possible box representation `[↑↓] [↑] [↑]`. Which equal-energy p orbital is drawn paired is not a distinct chemical species.

Sodium has eleven electrons: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s¹. Add the exponents to check 2 + 2 + 6 + 1 = 11. Collecting by n gives shell totals 2, 8, 1; shorthand [Ne] 3s¹ replaces the ten inner electrons. Shell totals omit subshell detail and cannot show Hund's arrangement.

An incorrect oxygen diagram `[↑↓] [↑↓] [ ]` obeys the two-electron limit but fails the ground-state Hund arrangement. A pair `[↑↑]` within one orbital fails Pauli. Diagnose which rule is broken rather than calling both mistakes simply “wrong filling”.

## Exceptions and applications
The n = 3 shell can hold 18 electrons, but argon ends at 3p⁶ with shell totals 2, 8, 8. Its empty 3d states do not invalidate the capacity formula. The next neutral ground-state atoms introduce 4s occupation before the transition series fills 3d. Electronic configuration underlies periodic behaviour and later bonding, but an isolated-atom box diagram alone does not predict every property of a metal bound in a protein.

## Short concept checks
- Which rule is broken by three electrons in a single orbital? **Answer:** Pauli's exclusion principle; only two opposite-spin electrons can share that orbital.
- Why is 2p³ drawn with three parallel single occupancies before a pair is formed? **Answer:** Hund's rule applies to the three degenerate p orbitals in the ground state.

## Key takeaways
Count electrons, use an appropriate energy order, enforce Pauli within an orbital and Hund across degenerate orbitals. Check the exponent total and distinguish shell capacity from actual occupation.

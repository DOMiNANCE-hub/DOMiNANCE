# Reversible inhibition: competitive, mixed and uncompetitive models

## Learning goals
Compare ideal reversible inhibition patterns, calculate apparent parameters with supplied factors and distinguish a kinetic pattern from a proven structural binding site.

## Define the model before assigning a label
An inhibitor decreases a measured catalytic rate under specified conditions. For an ideal single-substrate Michaelis–Menten system with reversible inhibitor binding, a useful mixed-inhibition expression is v=Vmax[S]/(αKm+α′[S]), where α=1+[I]/Ki describes the free-enzyme component and α′=1+[I]/Ki′ the ES-binding component. The simple model assumes known available inhibitor concentrations and suitable equilibrium/steady-state conditions; it does not cover every tight-binding, partial or time-dependent case.

Rearrangement gives apparent Vmax=Vmax/α′ and apparent Km=αKm/α′. Keeping the two factors separate prevents treating every non-active-site inhibitor as the same kinetic type. A kinetic label describes observations within a model; it does not by itself locate the inhibitor atom by atom.

## Competitive inhibition
In the ideal competitive model α>1 and α′=1. Substrate and inhibitor binding are mutually exclusive in the model, often through overlapping active-site interactions. Apparent Km increases by α, while Vmax is unchanged. Sufficiently high substrate can approach the original limiting rate at fixed available inhibitor, although practical concentrations or other mechanisms may prevent full experimental restoration.

For supplied Vmax=100 µM/min, Km=10 µM and α=3, at [S]=10 µM the inhibited rate is 100×10/(30+10)=25 µM/min versus uninhibited 50. At high substrate both ideal curves approach 100. A rise in apparent Km is not automatically evidence that the inhibitor chemically altered the enzyme's intrinsic equilibrium binding constant.

## Mixed and pure noncompetitive inhibition
In general mixed inhibition both factors exceed one and need not be equal. Apparent Vmax decreases, while apparent Km may increase or decrease depending on α/α′. Pure noncompetitive inhibition is the special case α=α′: apparent Km is unchanged and Vmax falls. The word “noncompetitive” should not be used as a blanket synonym for all mixed or allosteric effects.

With Vmax=100 and Km=10 in the same units, α=α′=2 gives apparent Vmax=50 and Km=10. At [S]=10, rate=25. If α=2 and α′=4 instead, apparent Vmax=25 and Km=5. High substrate cannot restore the original Vmax in either of these ideal cases. Equal affinity for free E and ES is part of the pure noncompetitive model; binding somewhere away from the active site alone does not prove it.

## Uncompetitive inhibition
An ideal uncompetitive inhibitor binds ES in the model, not free E: α=1 and α′>1. Both apparent Vmax and Km decrease by the same factor. For α′=2, apparent Vmax=50 and Km=5 from the example's original 100 and 10. At [S]=10, v=50×10/(5+10)≈33.3 µM/min. This lower apparent Km does not prove productive substrate binding became intrinsically stronger; inhibitor stabilisation of an inactive ES-containing population affects the kinetics.

## Comparing ideal patterns and evidence
| Pattern | Apparent Vmax | Apparent Km |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Competitive | Unchanged | Increased |
| Pure noncompetitive | Decreased | Unchanged |
| Uncompetitive | Decreased | Decreased by the same factor |
| Mixed | Decreased | Increased or decreased depending on factors |

In reciprocal plots, ideal competitive lines share a y-intercept; pure noncompetitive lines share an x-intercept; uncompetitive lines are parallel. These are model consequences, not substitutes for controlled concentration series, uncertainty analysis and interference checks. A single reduced rate cannot distinguish the mechanisms.

## Short concept checks
- Does every inhibitor binding outside the active site necessarily produce pure noncompetitive kinetics? **Answer:** No. Pure noncompetitive behaviour is a particular parameter relationship, not simply a site label.
- In ideal uncompetitive inhibition, do both apparent Km and Vmax decrease? **Answer:** Yes, by the same factor in the specified simple model.

## Key takeaways
Use the complete kinetic model. Competitive, mixed, pure noncompetitive and uncompetitive patterns differ; apparent parameters do not independently prove a structural mechanism.

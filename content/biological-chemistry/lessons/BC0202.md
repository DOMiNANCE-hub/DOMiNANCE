# Habitability and evidence for origin models

## Learning goals
Separate conditions that permit chemistry from evidence that life exists. Distinguish modern atmospheric conditions from uncertain early-Earth models.

## Conditions permit possibilities
Liquid water provides a solvent in which many biological reactions and transport processes can occur. A suitable energy supply, chemical ingredients and conditions that preserve relevant structures also matter. Temperature, pressure, radiation and local chemistry interact: a planet's distance from a star alone cannot establish surface liquid water or living organisms.

Habitability describes a possibility for life under specified assumptions. It is not detection of life. Finding organic molecules is also insufficient, because nonliving chemical processes can produce them. A strong argument distinguishes the observation, the proposed mechanism and alternative explanations.

Present-day oxygen-dependent organisms do not establish that free oxygen was required for the first life. Many organisms use anaerobic metabolism. Models of early Earth consider atmospheric and local environmental conditions that differed from today's, and the composition and timing remain subjects of investigation. A single strongly reducing gas mixture used in an experiment is one tested scenario, not a measurement of the entire early atmosphere.

Conditions may vary within one planet: a shoreline, mineral surface, hydrothermal setting and atmosphere offer different concentrations, energy sources and challenges. A proposal must explain how relevant reactants meet, how products persist and how destructive processes are limited. Saying that an environment contains energy does not show that the energy couples productively to a particular reaction.

## Worked claim evaluation
A report states that a planet lies in a region where liquid water might exist. The supported claim concerns a model-based possibility. Surface pressure, atmosphere and actual water are further questions. Even verified water would not establish metabolism, heredity or cells. Each step needs evidence rather than a chain of assumed conclusions.

Similarly, detecting amino acids in a nonbiological setting supports the availability of some organic precursors. It does not establish a complete protein, a replicating genome or a living cell.

## Source cautions
Specific planet rankings, old exoplanet claims and statements that all other planets lack life are not assessed here. They require current astronomical evidence and careful definitions. Oxygen is not treated as a universal prerequisite for life, and the lecturer's habitability list is used as a discussion framework rather than a definitive necessary-and-sufficient test.

## Short concept checks
- Does a potentially habitable orbit prove life exists? **Answer:** No. It constrains possible conditions, while actual conditions and biological evidence need separate investigation.
- Why should an origin experiment state its gas mixture? **Answer:** Products depend on starting conditions; the experiment tests that specified chemical scenario.

## Key takeaways
Habitability, organic chemistry and life detection are distinct claims. Evaluate assumptions and alternatives, and retain uncertainty about early environments where evidence remains incomplete.

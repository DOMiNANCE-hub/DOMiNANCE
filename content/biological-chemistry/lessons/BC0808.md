# Turnover, specificity constants and catalytic-centre counts

## Learning goals
Calculate kcat and kcat/Km with consistent units, distinguish maximal turnover from low-substrate performance and account for multiple catalytic centres in one enzyme molecule.

## Vmax depends on how much active enzyme is present
For a suitable Michaelis–Menten model, Vmax=kcatET when ET is the molar concentration of active catalytic centres. kcat has units of reciprocal time, commonly s−1. It describes turnovers per catalytic centre per unit time under saturating substrate and the specified conditions. It is not itself the amount of product per minute produced by an arbitrary tube of enzyme.

If an enzyme molecule contains more than one equivalent active centre, distinguish molecular concentration from centre concentration. A 10 nM dimer with two active sites per dimer provides 20 nM centres if both sites are functional. Using 10 nM without that qualification would double the inferred per-centre kcat. Inactive material, imperfect purity or an unknown active fraction can also distort a value inferred from total protein.

## Worked turnover calculation
Suppose Vmax=6 µmol L−1 min−1=6 µM/min and active-centre concentration is 20 nM=0.020 µM. Then kcat=6/0.020=300 min−1=5 s−1. The result is not 300 s−1, because the original rate used minutes. Alternatively convert Vmax to 0.10 µM/s first and divide by 0.020 µM to obtain 5 s−1.

If the same rate came from 10 nM dimers, each with two equivalent active centres, the centre-based answer stays 5 s−1. Reporting turnover per whole dimer would instead count ten product events per dimer per second in this illustrative equivalent-site model. State the denominator rather than calling both numbers contradictory.

## Low-substrate performance requires another quantity
When [S] is much smaller than Km, v0≈(kcat/Km)ET[S]. The specificity constant kcat/Km has units M−1 s−1. It combines productive turnover with the substrate dependence of the fitted model and can help compare competing substrates under suitable conditions. A high kcat alone does not establish efficient reaction at low substrate; a large Km can reduce the low-substrate slope.

The lecturer calls kcat “kinetic efficiency”. Here, turnover and the specificity constant are distinguished explicitly. Nor is the source's claim that catalase holds a universal all-time turnover record needed for understanding the mechanism; no such ranking is assessed. Numerical comparisons depend on definitions, enzyme form, substrate and assay conditions.

## Worked comparison that changes with substrate
Let enzyme A have kcat=100 s−1 and Km=100 µM=1×10−4 M. Its specificity constant is 1×10^6 M−1 s−1. Let enzyme B have kcat=20 s−1 and Km=1 µM=1×10−6 M. Its specificity constant is 2×10^7 M−1 s−1, twentyfold larger, even though its saturated turnover is fivefold lower.

At equal active-centre concentration and substrate far below both Km values, B is predicted to have the higher rate. At saturating substrate for both, A has the greater per-centre turnover. One ranking cannot answer both questions. Actual substrates may compete, and an in vivo rate also depends on enzyme abundance, compartments and other reaction partners.

## Avoid interpreting a ratio as a complete mechanism
A large specificity constant reflects productive encounters under the stated model, not proof that every collision succeeds. Diffusion, orientation, conformational changes and chemical steps can matter. Changes in kcat/Km alone do not identify which atomic step changed. Rate measurements must be interpreted alongside structural and chemical evidence.

## Short concept checks
- Why convert 300 min−1 before reporting s−1? **Answer:** The time units differ; divide by 60 to obtain 5 s−1.
- Can an enzyme with lower kcat outperform another at sufficiently low substrate? **Answer:** Yes. A larger kcat/Km can give a steeper low-substrate rate dependence at equal active-centre concentration.

## Key takeaways
Name the active-centre denominator and units. Saturated turnover and low-substrate specificity are distinct comparisons.

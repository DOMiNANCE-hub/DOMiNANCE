# Noncoding RNAs, processing and regulation

## Learning goals
Distinguish noncoding RNA functions, explain snRNA-associated splicing at an introductory level, and avoid treating noncoding as synonymous with useless or every transcript as proven functional.

## Coding potential and molecular function are separate
A functional noncoding RNA is not translated into a protein product in the role being described. tRNA and rRNA are central examples, not minor exceptions. Other classes include snRNAs, snoRNAs, miRNAs, siRNAs, piRNAs and many long noncoding RNAs. The lecturer's noRNAs label is unclear; the standard snoRNA class is taught as a clarification, without claiming that the source defines a separate noRNA class.

Naming a transcript noncoding does not establish a specific function by itself; experimental evidence is needed. Conversely, not being translated does not make an RNA biologically irrelevant. Coding mRNAs also contain untranslated regions, which are parts of an mRNA rather than necessarily separate functional ncRNA molecules.

## snRNA and pre-mRNA processing
In eukaryotic processing, introns can be removed and exons joined in the production of many mature mRNAs. Spliceosomal snRNAs interact with proteins and RNA substrates to organise recognition and catalysis. This differs from a ribosome translating codons into amino acids. Not every transcript has the same introns, and not every organism processes RNA in an identical way.

The source uses hnRNA and pre-mRNA as near synonyms but notes that heterogeneous nuclear RNA can include transcripts not destined to become cytoplasmic mRNA. That qualification matters: nuclear location alone does not prove future protein coding. Likewise, the source's approximately 150-nucleotide snRNA description is illustrative rather than an exact length requirement for every snRNA class.

## Regulatory and modification examples
snoRNAs help guide processing or modification of other RNAs, notably rRNA in familiar examples. miRNAs and siRNAs can guide sequence-dependent regulatory processes involving effector proteins; their outcomes depend on complementarity and system. Some guide complexes promote target cleavage, while other interactions alter translation or stability. It is too broad to say every small RNA destroys every mRNA it contacts.

Different classes can be discussed by origin, size, partners or role. The abbreviation sRNA is context-dependent: historically it could mean soluble tRNA, while small RNA is a broader modern usage in other contexts. Read the actual molecular role rather than treating every similar abbreviation as the same class.

## Short concept checks
- Is an intron-removing snRNA performing the same job as an amino-acid-carrying tRNA? **Answer:** No. Processing/splicing and translation-adaptor roles are distinct.
- Does calling a transcript noncoding prove it has no biological function? **Answer:** No. Many essential RNAs act without translation, although a particular transcript's function still requires evidence.

## Key takeaways
Classify by supported role and context. Coding status, cellular location, length and function are related observations rather than interchangeable definitions.

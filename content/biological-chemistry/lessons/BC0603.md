# Polynucleotide backbone, directionality and sequence

## Learning goals
Explain the 3′–5′ phosphodiester linkage, read antiparallel sequences and calculate backbone links under stated topology assumptions.

## Direction comes from asymmetric sugar positions
An ordinary nucleic-acid chain links the 3′ oxygen of one sugar through phosphate to the 5′ oxygen of the next. Each internal bridging phosphate is a phosphodiester because it has two ester connections. This covalent backbone differs from the base–sugar glycosidic bond and from noncovalent base pairing between strands.

A chain has chemically distinct 5′ and 3′ ends. The end groups can be modified or phosphorylated, so a 5′ end is not defined as always bearing one particular terminal phosphate state. Sequence notation conventionally reads 5′→3′ unless otherwise specified. Standard polymerase extension uses a growing 3′ OH and an incoming nucleotide triphosphate, extending the strand 5′→3′ while release of pyrophosphate accompanies incorporation. The incorporated residue does not retain its entire triphosphate chain at every internal link.

## Worked reverse-complement method
For the strand 5′-AGTC-3′, pair A with T, G with C, T with A and C with G. Directly opposite is 3′-TCAG-5′. To report that complementary strand in the usual 5′→3′ orientation, reverse its written order: 5′-GACT-3′. Complementing without reversing gives the wrong answer when both requested sequences must be written 5′→3′.

Antiparallel describes opposite backbone directions, not opposite electrical charges. Both strands are polyanionic. Complementary strands are not normally identical in sequence, although special symmetric sequences can match their own reverse complement. Sequence context and notation therefore matter more than memorising that one side always starts with a particular base.

## Worked bond and sequence counts
A linear single strand of n residues has n−1 internal phosphodiester links. A five-residue chain therefore has four; a covalently closed five-residue circle has five. Terminal phosphorylation is a different feature and does not add an internal link between two residues. Two separate ordinary linear five-residue strands have eight backbone links in total, regardless of their hydrogen-bond pairing.

For an oriented sequence of four independently chosen positions using A,T,G,C, there are 4⁴=256 possible strings. Once a canonical complementary strand is specified, its paired partner is determined rather than adding a second independent factor of 256. This is a stated combinatorial model, not a claim that DNA has literally infinite information for a finite length.

## Short concept checks
- What is the 5′→3′ reverse complement of 5′-AGTC-3′? **Answer:** 5′-GACT-3′; the directly opposite strand is first written 3′-TCAG-5′.
- Does separating a duplex necessarily break its phosphodiester backbones? **Answer:** No. Ordinary strand separation disrupts noncovalent organisation; covalent cleavage is a different process.

## Key takeaways
Track sugar numbering, strand direction and topology explicitly. Backbone bonds, glycosidic links and base-pair interactions answer different structural questions.

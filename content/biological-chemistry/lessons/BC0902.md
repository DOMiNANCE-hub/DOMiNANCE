# Direction, free energy and coupled pathway steps

## Learning goals
Explain conditional pathway direction, add reaction free energies, distinguish near-equilibrium steps from strongly displaced ones and qualify the meaning of a committed step.

## Direction depends on conditions, not an arrow alone
A pathway's net direction depends on its reactions and maintained concentrations, energy carriers and transport. ΔG describes the driving force under stated conditions. A negative value favours the forward direction; positive favours the reverse. At equilibrium ΔG=0 and net change vanishes, even though molecular reactions can continue. Enzymes affect kinetics rather than the equilibrium constant for the same chemical states.

The lecture calls pathways irreversible. A more precise description is that a functioning pathway often contains steps strongly displaced from equilibrium under particular cellular conditions. Some other steps are near equilibrium and can readily respond to changes in substrate/product ratios. “Physiologically irreversible” is conditional; it is not an assertion that reverse chemistry is impossible under every concentration or that every pathway must have the same architecture.

## Free energies add, but coupling needs chemistry
For consecutive balanced reactions A→B and B→C, B cancels from the net equation A→C and the reaction ΔG values add when defined under compatible conditions. If the first has supplied ΔG=+12 kJ/mol and the second −20 kJ/mol, the total is −8 kJ/mol. A favourable total can support forward conversion when the reactions are appropriately connected and concentrations permit; it does not make the first reaction's isolated ΔG automatically negative in every mixture.

For an endergonic transformation with supplied ΔG=+18 kJ/mol and an explicitly coupled carrier reaction ΔG=−30 kJ/mol, the combined balance is −12 kJ/mol. Merely putting ATP in the same vessel does not ensure coupling. A mechanism must connect the processes, for example through transfer to a shared activated intermediate. Counting how many ATP molecules appear without defining their reactions and conditions is insufficient to assign a universal energy yield.

## Concentration can change the direction
For the simple ideal reaction A⇌B, Q=[B]/[A] and ΔG=ΔG°′+RT ln Q under the stated biochemical convention. With supplied ΔG°′=+5.0 kJ/mol, RT=2.5 kJ/mol and Q=0.10, ΔG=5.0+2.5 ln(0.10)≈−0.76 kJ/mol. The standard-state value is positive while the actual value in this example is negative.

If Q becomes 10 with the same supplied values, ΔG≈+10.76 kJ/mol. All quantities are explicitly illustrative; a real reaction may involve more species, nonideal activities, pH effects and compartment-specific conditions. A substrate concentration by itself cannot set the direction without its reaction partners.

## Committed does not mean guaranteed completion
A first committed step is commonly the first reaction directing a shared intermediate into a particular route rather than several alternative fates. It can be an important regulatory point. However, commitment is defined relative to the pathway/network being considered, and its product can still face transport, inhibition, breakdown or an alternative fate. The lecture's “proceeds to completion” is not a guarantee for every molecule.

Opposing physiological routes often use distinct enzymes or bypasses around strongly displaced steps, permitting independent regulation. Not every step must be different. A pathway is not adequately explained by saying all anabolic and catabolic arrows can be reversed indiscriminately, or that every pathway has one universal permanently rate-determining reaction.

## Short concept checks
- What is the total of supplied compatible step free energies +12 and −20 kJ/mol? **Answer:** −8 kJ/mol for the defined net transformation, with coupling and conditions still specified.
- Does a positive standard-state ΔG°′ prove the forward reaction can never occur? **Answer:** No. Actual reactant/product activities can change ΔG and direction.

## Key takeaways
Specify net chemistry and conditions. Conditional irreversibility, coupling, commitment and regulation describe related but distinct features.

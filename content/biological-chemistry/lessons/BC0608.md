# RNA folding, mRNA, tRNA and rRNA

## Learning goals
Relate RNA sequence and folding to information transfer, adaptor function and ribosomal catalysis. Read codon/anticodon orientations without assuming that all RNAs encode proteins.

## Single-stranded does not mean structureless
Many cellular RNAs are single covalent strands with self-complementary regions that form stems, loops and more complex folds. Local base pairing and tertiary contacts create organised structures; RNA is not necessarily a long unstructured thread. Double-stranded RNA also occurs, including in viral systems. RNA often uses A,U,G,C, while modified residues and special contexts qualify an absolute thymine-never-in-RNA rule.

RNA's 2′ OH contributes to both folding interactions and particular cleavage pathways. Its presence alone does not establish the actual lifetime of a cellular RNA, which also depends on sequence, proteins, enzymes and environment. Comparisons with DNA should specify the property being considered rather than declaring one molecule universally better in every role.

## Messenger and adaptor roles
mRNA carries information used by ribosomes to specify a polypeptide sequence. In a defined reading frame, codons are three bases read 5′→3′. A 12-nucleotide coding segment contains four complete triplets if it starts at the given boundary; it need not make four amino acids if one triplet is a stop. Reading-frame and processing assumptions belong in calculations.

A tRNA links recognition of a codon to an attached amino acid. Its anticodon pairs antiparallel with the mRNA codon, while an aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase helps charge the correct amino acid onto the tRNA's 3′ end. Codon pairing alone does not chemically attach the amino acid. The familiar cloverleaf is a secondary-structure representation; folded tRNA has a three-dimensional L-like architecture.

For mRNA codon 5′-AUG-3′, a simple Watson–Crick anticodon is aligned 3′-UAC-5′, or written conventionally 5′→3′ as CAU. AUG specifies methionine in the standard code and can serve as a start in the appropriate context. Wobble and modified bases broaden some pairing relationships; a simple complementary triplet is not a complete account of every decoding event.

## Using the standard codon table
Read an mRNA codon 5′→3′. In the lecturer's chart, select the first base on the left, the second along the top and the third on the right. The original fact table below groups codons by their first two bases; choose the third-base column to complete the codon. It describes the standard code, not every mitochondrial or recoding exception.

| First two bases | Third U | Third C | Third A | Third G |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| UU | Phe | Phe | Leu | Leu |
| UC | Ser | Ser | Ser | Ser |
| UA | Tyr | Tyr | Stop | Stop |
| UG | Cys | Cys | Stop | Trp |
| CU | Leu | Leu | Leu | Leu |
| CC | Pro | Pro | Pro | Pro |
| CA | His | His | Gln | Gln |
| CG | Arg | Arg | Arg | Arg |
| AU | Ile | Ile | Ile | Met |
| AC | Thr | Thr | Thr | Thr |
| AA | Asn | Asn | Lys | Lys |
| AG | Ser | Ser | Arg | Arg |
| GU | Val | Val | Val | Val |
| GC | Ala | Ala | Ala | Ala |
| GA | Asp | Asp | Glu | Glu |
| GG | Gly | Gly | Gly | Gly |

Abbreviation key: Phe phenylalanine; Leu leucine; Ser serine; Tyr tyrosine; Cys cysteine; Trp tryptophan; Pro proline; His histidine; Gln glutamine; Arg arginine; Ile isoleucine; Met methionine; Thr threonine; Asn asparagine; Lys lysine; Val valine; Ala alanine; Asp aspartate; Glu glutamate; Gly glycine.

For example, UGG uses row UG and column G, giving tryptophan (Trp). GAA uses row GA and column A, giving glutamate (Glu). UAA, UAG and UGA are the three standard stop codons, which signal termination rather than specifying ordinary amino acids. With four choices at three positions there are 4³=64 possible triplets; subtracting three stops gives 61 sense codons in this standard comparison.

Degeneracy means several codons can specify the same amino acid: UUU and UUC both specify phenylalanine. It does not mean that a standard codon arbitrarily specifies multiple amino acids. The protein output also requires a reading frame, start context and a functional translation system; the table is not a claim that every triplet anywhere in an RNA is translated.

## Ribosomal structure includes catalytic RNA
rRNA contributes to ribosome structure, decoding organisation and catalysis. The large-subunit peptidyl-transferase centre is RNA-based, explaining why the ribosome is an important ribozyme example. Ribosomal proteins remain important; RNA catalysis does not mean the whole ribosome contains no proteins.

rRNA numbers, processing and composition differ among organisms: bacterial ribosomes commonly contain 16S,23S and 5S rRNAs, while eukaryotic cytosolic ribosomes contain 18S,28S,5.8S and 5S. Thus the source's two-major-rRNA statement and approximate RNA/protein mass fractions should not be treated as universal exact inventories. Conserved rRNA sequence features are useful in evolutionary comparison, but sequence differences and methodological context matter.

## Short concept checks
- Written 5′→3′, what simple anticodon complements mRNA AUG? **Answer:** CAU; the directly aligned anticodon is 3′-UAC-5′.
- Does rRNA catalysis mean ribosomes lack proteins? **Answer:** No. The catalytic centre is RNA-based within a ribonucleoprotein assembly.

## Key takeaways
RNA combines information and molecular structure. Distinguish coding sequence, adaptor charging, codon pairing and catalytic assembly.

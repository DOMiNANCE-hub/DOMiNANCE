# Cofactors, coenzymes and vitamin-derived chemical carriers

## Learning goals
Distinguish apoenzyme, holoenzyme, cofactor and prosthetic group; link representative vitamin precursors to their active carrier forms without treating all vitamins as equivalent cofactors.

## Nonprotein partners supply useful chemistry
A cofactor is a nonprotein component required for a particular enzyme's activity. It can be an appropriate metal ion or an organic coenzyme. The apoenzyme is the protein component without its required cofactor; the appropriately assembled active form is the holoenzyme. Whether removing a component leaves a stable, refoldable apoenzyme depends on the protein.

A tightly associated nonprotein group is often called a prosthetic group. Tight association need not mean covalent attachment. Other coenzymes act as exchangeable cosubstrates that bind, change chemical state and leave. Thus, “most coenzymes must remain permanently attached” is not a useful universal rule. Small nonprotein components are also not universally resistant to boiling, light or oxidation.

Metal ions can help bind substrates, stabilise charge or participate in redox chemistry. Mg2+ often associates with nucleotide phosphate groups in reactions using ATP. Zn2+ can support catalysis in carbonic anhydrase. Selenium in a selenoprotein residue is not simply a free metal ion: the lecturer's broad metal/cofactor inventory requires chemical distinctions rather than classifying every listed element as a metal.

## Redox carriers: NAD and flavins
Niacin, vitamin B3, contributes to NAD and NADP. A useful half-reaction is NAD+ + H+ + 2e− ⇌ NADH. In many dehydrogenase reactions a hydride, equivalent to H+ plus two electrons, is transferred to the nicotinamide ring, with other proton bookkeeping determined by the reaction. Write NADH rather than treating NADH2 as the standard reduced species.

NADP has an additional phosphate important in recognition; that phosphate does not mean NADPH automatically transfers a higher-energy electron than NADH. Many catabolic processes use NAD+/NADH, while NADPH commonly supplies reducing power for biosynthesis and antioxidant systems. These are broad functional trends, not universal ratios for every cell compartment.

Riboflavin, vitamin B2, gives FMN and FAD. Their flavin rings can participate in one- and two-electron chemistry. Many are tightly associated with enzymes; some FAD attachments are covalent. Binding mode must therefore be checked rather than asserting that every flavin is noncovalent. Reoxidation regenerates the carrier for further cycles.

## Group-transfer carriers
| Precursor | Representative coenzyme | Useful chemical role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thiamine B1 | TPP | Stabilises intermediates in α-keto-acid decarboxylation and selected carbon-unit transfers |
| Pantothenate B5 | Coenzyme A | Carries acyl groups through a reactive thiol/thioester system |
| Vitamin B6 forms | PLP | Supports amino-acid reactions including transamination |
| Biotin B7 | Enzyme-linked biotin | Carries activated CO2 in carboxylase reactions |
| Folate B9 | Tetrahydrofolate derivatives | Carry one-carbon units in specified oxidation states |
| Vitamin B12 | Methyl- or adenosylcobalamin | Distinct methyl-transfer or rearrangement chemistry, respectively |

Biotin can be covalently attached through a lysine side chain, allowing its carrier group to move between catalytic sites. It is not equivalent to THF merely because both handle carbon: CO2 transfer and other one-carbon chemistries differ. TPP helps stabilise reactive carbon intermediates rather than acting as a generic source of ATP energy. PLP facilitates amino-group chemistry; it does not create every missing carbon skeleton.

## Worked regeneration logic
If an enzyme reduces one NAD+ to NADH per substrate oxidised, a supplied pool of ten NAD+ molecules cannot support unlimited net substrate oxidation unless NADH is reoxidised or additional NAD+ is supplied. The enzyme may be regenerated each catalytic cycle while a cosubstrate pool changes state. “Catalyst regenerated” is not permission to omit a required coenzyme-regeneration pathway.

## Short concept checks
- Must a prosthetic group be covalently linked? **Answer:** No. Tight functional association can also be noncovalent.
- Are biotin-mediated CO2 transfer and every THF one-carbon transfer the same reaction? **Answer:** No. The carbon species, carrier chemistry and enzyme context differ.

## Key takeaways
Name the active coenzyme and transferred species. Binding mode, regeneration and reaction context matter; no vitamin dose or deficiency diagnosis follows from this inventory.

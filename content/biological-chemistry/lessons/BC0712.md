# Choosing and interpreting protein-separation methods

## Learning goals
Match separation methods to size, charge or specific binding, predict simple elution behaviour, and distinguish a purified-looking sample from proven identity and activity.

## A purification workflow needs a property and a readout
A cell extract contains many proteins and other molecules. A useful workflow exploits a property that differs between the target and contaminants while retaining the required structure or activity. Fractions must be tested: detecting total protein does not identify the desired protein. Activity assays, molecular analysis and controls provide complementary information.

Size-exclusion chromatography uses porous beads. Larger molecules excluded from many pores take a shorter accessible path and tend to elute earlier; smaller molecules explore more pore volume and elute later. This is not the same as large proteins binding more tightly to an affinity resin. Shape, aggregation and the column's fractionation range affect apparent size, so an early peak alone does not prove a particular sequence or exact mass. Ultrafiltration uses a membrane and an approximate molecular-size cutoff, while sedimentation methods also depend on density and shape, not only residue count.

## Ion exchange depends on the resin and buffer
A cation exchanger carries fixed negative groups and binds sufficiently positive solutes; an anion exchanger carries fixed positive groups and binds sufficiently negative solutes. The name identifies the exchanged solute ion, not the charge attached to the resin. Below its pI a protein tends to be net positive, and above its pI net negative. Actual binding also depends on surface-charge distribution, salt, resin chemistry and pH-dependent ionisation of the resin.

For a hypothetical protein with pI 8 in a pH 6 buffer, positive net charge makes binding to a suitable cation exchanger plausible. A protein with pI 4 in that buffer tends negative and may bind an anion exchanger instead. At pH near pI, weak net charge can reduce binding and solubility, but neither zero binding nor precipitation is inevitable.

Increasing competing salt ions or changing pH can weaken ion-exchange retention and elute bound material. A universal elution order cannot be inferred without specifying conditions. HPLC describes a high-performance chromatographic implementation; it is not automatically a single chemical separation principle shared by every HPLC column.

## Affinity, electrophoresis and salt fractionation
Affinity purification uses a suitably selective interaction, such as target binding to an immobilised ligand. Binding, washing and controlled elution enrich the target, but nonspecific proteins can remain. Harsh elution may damage function; specificity and yield require measurement.

Native electrophoretic movement depends on net charge, size and shape. SDS treatment largely unfolds many proteins and supplies a broadly similar negative charge-to-mass ratio, making SDS-PAGE commonly useful for estimating polypeptide size. Reduction additionally breaks susceptible disulfides. A multimer can therefore yield smaller bands even though no peptide bonds were deliberately hydrolysed. One visible band is supportive evidence of limited detectable heterogeneity, not definitive proof of identity, complete purity or native activity.

Low concentrations of suitable salts can improve some proteins' solubility through screening; high concentrations of particular salts can promote salting out. Protein, ion identity, pH and temperature matter. The lecture's <0.5 M and >1 M divisions are illustrative rather than universal thresholds. Salt precipitation may be reversible and need not imply backbone cleavage or irreversible denaturation.

## Worked recovery and activity example
Suppose an extract contains 100 mg total protein and 200 activity units. Its specific activity is 200/100=2 units/mg. A fraction contains 10 mg and 100 units: specific activity=10 units/mg, fivefold enrichment, with activity recovery 100/200×100=50%. Lower total mass alone would not demonstrate purification; increased activity per mass supplies the relevant evidence under the assay assumptions. The units are explicitly supplied, not invented experimental results.

A useful next step depends on what remains uncertain. A second charge-based separation may be less informative than an independent size or affinity step when contaminants share charge. Choosing a method is a reasoned prediction that must be checked experimentally; no laboratory purification was performed for this lesson.

## Short concept checks
- Which exchanger has a positively charged resin and binds anions? **Answer:** An anion exchanger; the name describes the retained/exchanged solute charge.
- Does fivefold higher specific activity mean 100% target recovery? **Answer:** No. In the worked example enrichment is fivefold while activity recovery is only 50%.

## Key takeaways
State the separation property, conditions and readout. Charge, size, specificity, identity, purity, recovery and retained activity answer different questions.

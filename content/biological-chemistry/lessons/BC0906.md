# Enzyme induction, allostery and covalent pathway switches

## Learning goals
Compare regulation by abundance and existing-protein activity, explain the lac-operon example with its missing qualifications and distinguish different covalent modifications.

## Changing the amount differs from changing each molecule
Enzyme synthesis and degradation can alter pathway capacity over time. Induction increases expression in response to a signal; repression reduces it. Changes require transcription, translation and suitable maturation/turnover, with timescales depending on organism and conditions. A precursor already present in the cell may instead be activated rapidly without producing more protein.

The lecturer's bacterial substrate-induction example illustrates adaptation to available nutrients. It is not a rule that every added substrate always induces a matching enzyme or that every cell has the same on/off genetic logic. Some substrates act indirectly, while other signals coordinate several routes at once.

## Reading the lac example correctly
In the familiar E. coli lac system, a repressor can occupy the operator and reduce transcription of lactose-use genes. Allolactose, formed in the presence of lactose, binds the repressor and reduces its operator association. This is more precise than describing only “lactose present means fully on”. The repressor does not need to disappear permanently for regulation to occur.

Glucose availability also matters. In the simplified regulatory model, low glucose favours cAMP–CAP/CRP-mediated activation of transcription, while high glucose reduces that activation. Lactose availability and glucose status therefore provide separate controls. Relief of repression is not identical to maximal transcription under every condition. This is a supported interpretation of the lecturer's schematic, not development of an additional genetics course.

## Activity control acts on an existing protein
Allosteric ligands can alter conformation and function at a regulatory site, often on a shorter timescale than new synthesis. Binding a ligand does not automatically make the inhibitor a pure noncompetitive kinetic case; Unit 8 explains why a full rate model is needed. A ligand may be a small metabolite or another protein; the source's awkward “low molecular weight polymers” wording is not adopted as a definition.

Covalent modifications add or remove specific groups. Kinases transfer phosphoryl groups; phosphatases usually remove them by hydrolysis. Effects depend on the particular enzyme/site: phosphate is not a universal inactivation mark. AMP attachment in adenylylation and ADP-ribosyl transfer are chemically distinct modifications, using different donor groups and linkages. Mentioning ATP or NAD+ does not make those processes interchangeable with oxidation or phosphorylation.

## Worked abundance-versus-state comparison
Let a hypothetical enzyme population contain 100 equivalent units, of which 25% are active. An allosteric or covalent response raises the active fraction to 50% without changing abundance: active units rise from 25 to 50. Separately, synthesis doubles abundance to 200 while the active fraction stays at 25%: active units also become 50. Identical isolated capacity changes can therefore arise by different mechanisms.

The cellular flux need not double in either case if transport, substrate or another process limits the response. Tests of expression, modification state and pathway flow provide different evidence. A total protein measurement alone cannot distinguish the two example interventions.

## Short concept checks
- Why is “lactose present means maximal lac transcription” incomplete? **Answer:** Allolactose/repressor control and glucose-dependent CAP/CRP activation both influence the familiar model.
- Can enzyme activity increase without a change in total enzyme abundance? **Answer:** Yes. Changing the active fraction or per-molecule behaviour can regulate an existing population.

## Key takeaways
Distinguish signal, protein amount, chemical modification and system flux. A simplified operon figure requires qualified interpretation, not unconditional on/off claims.

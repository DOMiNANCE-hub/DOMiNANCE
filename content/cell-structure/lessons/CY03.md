# CY03 — Microtubule structure, polarity and dynamic instability

## Learning mission
Describe the structure of a microtubule and explain how polarity, assembly and disassembly support intracellular transport.

## Core lesson
The lecturer describes microtubules as **stiff but flexible** and notes that human axons can extend **more than one metre**; microtubules serve as transport tracks over these distances.

A **microtubule** is a hollow, roughly **25 nm**-wide cylinder built from **α- and β-tubulin** subunits. In a typical cytoplasmic microtubule, **13 protofilaments** align around the wall of the tube. A protofilament is a linear chain of repeating α/β-tubulin dimers.

Because α- and β-tubulin are arranged consistently, microtubules have distinct **plus (+)** and **minus (−)** ends. These labels describe structural polarity, not electrical charge. The plus end often changes length more rapidly, although behavior depends on cellular regulation. Assembly requires sufficient available tubulin and appropriate conditions; the lecture highlights a **critical concentration** of tubulin.

**Dynamic instability** is the switching of individual microtubule ends between growth and shrinkage. Tubulin bound to GTP is incorporated into a growing microtubule; GTP hydrolysis influences stability. Loss of a stabilizing GTP-rich end region can trigger rapid shortening (catastrophe), while renewed growth is called rescue. This explains why microtubules can maintain tracks yet rapidly reorganize during mitosis.

A **microtubule-organizing centre (MTOC)** helps nucleate and organize microtubules. In many animal cells, the centrosome is a major MTOC. Microtubules also provide tracks for long-distance axonal transport.

**Microtubules and Alzheimer's disease:** the lecture describes tau as a microtubule-associated protein (MAP), and relates abnormal tau aggregation and microtubule disruption to impaired neuronal transport. Important qualification: Alzheimer's disease is multifactorial; tau pathology is one major component, not a complete single-cause explanation.

## Slide Essentials
- Microtubule: stiff but flexible hollow cylinder, ~25 nm diameter, usually 13 protofilaments.
- Axonal transport may extend along axons more than one metre long.
- Building block: α/β-tubulin dimer.
- Ends: structurally distinct plus and minus ends.
- Dynamic instability: alternating growth and shrinkage; important for spindle rearrangement.
- Tau is a microtubule-associated protein; altered tau is relevant to neuronal disease.

## Concept Connections
**Protofilament** = linear tubulin polymer; **polarity** = directional asymmetry of filament structure; **MTOC** = site organizing microtubule growth; **tau** = neuronal MAP that interacts with microtubules.

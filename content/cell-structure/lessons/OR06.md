# OR06 — Nucleolus, Chromatin and Chromosomes

**Source:** BIOL 151 Cell Structure, PDF pages 235–240.

**Learning Mission:** Explain the defining structure, major functions, and key distinctions in this lesson.

## The nucleolus
The **nucleolus** is a prominent, **membrane-less** region of the nucleus where **ribosomal RNA (rRNA)** is produced and **ribosomal subunits** are assembled. Ribosomal proteins are made in the cytoplasm and imported into the nucleus; they combine with rRNA to form subunits that are later exported.

The nucleolus commonly appears as a dark-staining region under a light microscope. It forms around **nucleolar organiser regions (NORs)**, chromosome regions containing rRNA genes. During typical open mitosis, nucleolar organisation disassembles and is re-established afterward. The slide mentions up to four nucleoli and a fixed number for each species; **clarification:** nucleolar number varies with cell type, activity and cell-cycle state, so a universal fixed count should not be memorised as a rule.

The slides also mention possible roles of the nucleolus in **cellular ageing/senescence**. This is a research connection, not its principal textbook function.

## Chromatin versus chromosomes
**Chromatin** refers to DNA and associated proteins in the nucleus. **Chromosomes** are discrete DNA–protein structures; they are especially visible when chromatin condenses during division. Chromatin does not disappear outside mitosis, and chromosomes are not newly created only when a cell divides.

**Nucleosomes** provide a basic level of DNA packaging. **Histones** are basic proteins that associate with negatively charged DNA. **Euchromatin** is generally less condensed and more accessible; **heterochromatin** is generally more condensed, though activity depends on context. These latter terms support interpretation of the diagram slides.

## Concept Connections
**rRNA** is a structural/catalytic component of ribosomes; **ribosomal subunit** combines rRNA and proteins; **NOR** contains rRNA gene clusters; **senescence** is a durable loss of cell-division capacity; **euchromatin/heterochromatin** refer to comparatively open/compact chromatin.

# Lesson 10 — Fluorescence and Immunofluorescence

### Fluorescence microscopy

**Fluorescence** occurs when a substance absorbs light of one wavelength and emits light at a longer wavelength. A **fluorescence microscope** uses illumination and filters to excite fluorescent substances and detect the light they emit.

Some cellular substances show **natural fluorescence**. Other structures can be labelled with fluorescent dyes called **fluorochromes**. The labelled structures then appear bright against a relatively dark background.

### Excitation and emission

1. **Excitation light** illuminates a fluorescent molecule and raises it to a higher-energy state.
2. The molecule returns towards a lower-energy state and emits light at a **longer wavelength**.
3. Optical filters help ensure that mainly the emitted light reaches the observer or camera.

A **dichroic mirror** separates excitation light from emitted fluorescence by reflecting selected wavelengths and transmitting others.

### Epifluorescence

In **epifluorescence microscopy**, the **objective lens** (the lens nearest the specimen) both directs excitation light towards the specimen and collects the emitted fluorescent light. The microscope must separate the incoming and outgoing light efficiently to avoid a bright background obscuring the signal.

### Immunofluorescence

**Immunofluorescence** uses an **antibody** that binds a particular cellular protein and carries a fluorescent label, such as **fluorescein**. When the labelled antibody binds its target, the corresponding structure fluoresces under suitable illumination.

This allows specific molecules or cell structures to be located rather than merely showing the outline of a cell.

### Slide Essentials
- Fluorescence: absorption of shorter-wavelength light followed by emission at a longer wavelength.
- **Fluorochromes** are fluorescent dyes used to label otherwise non-fluorescent structures.
- **Epifluorescence:** objective lens both focuses excitation light and collects emitted fluorescence.
- **Dichroic mirror:** separates excitation and emission light paths.
- **Immunofluorescence:** fluorescently labelled antibodies identify specific proteins or structures; **fluorescein** is an example of a label.

### Concept Connections
**UV microscopy → shorter-wavelength illumination.** **Fluorescence → excitation followed by longer-wavelength emission.** **Dichroic mirror + filters → clearer fluorescent signal.** **Antigen–antibody binding → selective identification of cellular proteins.**

**Optional check:** What is the role of a dichroic mirror? *It helps separate excitation light from fluorescence emission.*

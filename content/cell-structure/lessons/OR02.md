# OR02 — Glyoxysomes and Hydrogenosomes

**Source:** BIOL 151 Cell Structure, PDF pages 210–213.

**Learning Mission:** Explain the defining structure, major functions, and key distinctions in this lesson.

## Glyoxysomes
**Glyoxysomes** are specialised plant microbodies, especially important in **germinating oil-rich seeds**. They contain enzymes of the **glyoxylate cycle**, allowing carbon from stored fatty acids to contribute to carbohydrate production.

The sequence presented by the lecture is: **stored fats → acetyl-CoA → glyoxylate-cycle intermediates → net succinate → pathways contributing to sugar formation**. This supports transport of energy reserves to growing seed tissues. **Scientific clarification:** succinate itself is not the immediate substrate of gluconeogenesis; it must first be converted through intermediary metabolic reactions. The slide's wording about transport to mitochondria and subsequent gluconeogenesis is a simplified pathway overview.

## Hydrogenosomes
A **hydrogenosome** is a membrane-enclosed energy-producing organelle found in some **anaerobic eukaryotes**, including certain trichomonads, ciliates and fungi. It can release **molecular hydrogen (H₂)** as a by-product of energy metabolism under oxygen-poor conditions. It is related evolutionarily to mitochondria, but its metabolic pathways differ from conventional aerobic mitochondrial respiration.

## Glycosomes: listed but not explained in the slides
The lecture names **glycosomes** among microbodies without providing their detailed biochemistry. As supplementary clarification, glycosomes compartmentalise much of glycolysis in some protozoa, notably kinetoplastids; do not confuse them with glycogen granules.

## Slide Essentials
Glyoxysome—plant seed lipid-to-carbohydrate conversion via glyoxylate cycle; net succinate from acetyl-CoA; hydrogenosome—anaerobic eukaryote organelle releasing H₂; glycosome—named in the lecture but not developed.

## Concept Connections
**Glyoxylate cycle** bypasses carbon-loss steps of the citric-acid cycle; **gluconeogenesis** forms glucose from non-carbohydrate precursors; **anaerobic** means without reliance on oxygen; **H₂** is molecular hydrogen, not hydrogen peroxide.

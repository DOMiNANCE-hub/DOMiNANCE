# Lesson 17 — Staining for Light Microscopy

### Why stain tissues?

Many tissue sections are almost transparent. **Staining** increases contrast so that cells and their components can be distinguished.

Common light-microscopy stains include organic dyes. Some, such as **toluidine blue**, stain broadly; others preferentially bind to particular cell components.

### Basic and acidic stains

Stain behaviour depends partly on **electrical charge**.

| Stain type | Charge of dye component | Main binding tendency | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Basic stain** | Positively charged (**cationic**) | Negatively charged structures, including nucleic acids | Haematoxylin (in this course’s classification), crystal violet, methylene blue, safranin, basic fuchsin |
| **Acidic stain** | Negatively charged (**anionic**) | Positively charged groups, especially many cytoplasmic proteins | Eosin, nigrosine, picric acid, acid fuchsin, India ink (listed among examples) |

**Understanding note:** Haematoxylin is commonly used with a mordant to form a positively charged staining complex; the practical result is strong nuclear staining. Some listed materials, including India ink, are better understood as specialised contrast agents rather than simple acidic dyes.

### Haematoxylin and eosin (H&E)

**Haematoxylin** preferentially colours nuclei blue-purple because of their nucleic-acid-rich material. **Eosin** colours many cytoplasmic and extracellular proteins pink to red. Together, they form a routine stain combination for many animal tissues.

### Concept Connections

**Charge → selective staining:** opposite charges attract. **Nucleic acids → nuclear basophilia:** nuclei bind the haematoxylin staining complex. **Protein-rich cytoplasm → eosinophilia:** cytoplasmic components often bind eosin. **Staining → contrast:** dyes make transparent sections interpretable.

### Slide Essentials

- Broad stain: **toluidine blue**.
- **Basic stains** bind mainly negatively charged structures; **acidic stains** bind mainly positively charged structures.
- Basic examples: **haematoxylin, crystal violet, methylene blue, safranin, basic fuchsin**.
- Acidic examples listed: **nigrosine, picric acid, eosin, acid fuchsin, India ink**.
- **H&E** is routinely used for animal tissues; nuclei blue-purple and much cytoplasm pink-red.

**Optional check:** Why does eosin commonly stain cytoplasm? *Its negatively charged dye binds positively charged groups in cytoplasmic proteins.*

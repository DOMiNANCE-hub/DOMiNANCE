# CY04 — Microtubules in mitosis and medicine

## Learning mission
Explain why disrupting microtubule dynamics can prevent cell division and distinguish stabilizing from destabilizing drugs.

## Core lesson
During **mitosis**, microtubules form the spindle apparatus. Spindle microtubules help align chromosomes and move them toward opposite poles. These activities require controlled assembly, disassembly and motor-driven movement. A cell may fail to complete division if its spindle cannot reorganize correctly.

This makes microtubules an important target in cancer treatment. The lecture distinguishes two broad pharmacological strategies:

1. **Prevent microtubule assembly or promote disassembly.** This reduces functional spindle formation. The lecture names **colchicine, nocodazole and vincristine** as drugs that inhibit assembly or favor depolymerization.
2. **Stabilize microtubules and inhibit disassembly.** This prevents the normal rearrangements required for spindle function. **Taxol (paclitaxel)** is the lecture's example.

Both excessive stabilization and excessive destabilization can interfere with mitosis. The goal is not simply to destroy every microtubule, since normal cells also need them. Clinical usefulness depends on therapeutic benefit, selectivity, dosing and adverse effects. The lecturer specifically emphasizes the challenge of limiting damage to **non-cancerous cells** and achieving **safe, effective delivery** with minimal general side effects.

**Clinical precision:** vincristine and paclitaxel are established anticancer drugs. Colchicine and nocodazole are useful mechanistic examples in the lecture, but they should not all be presented as interchangeable routine cancer therapies.

## Slide Essentials
- Mitotic spindle: microtubule system involved in chromosome alignment and segregation.
- **Taxol/paclitaxel**: stabilizes microtubules and inhibits normal disassembly.
- **Vincristine**: interferes with microtubule assembly; used in cancer chemotherapy.
- **Colchicine and nocodazole**: inhibit microtubule polymerization (important lecture examples).
- Both blocking polymerization and blocking depolymerization can disrupt cell division.
- The lecture emphasizes effects on healthy cells, safe delivery, and systemic side effects.

## Concept Connections
**Polymerization** = building a filament; **depolymerization** = breaking it down; **spindle** = microtubule-based chromosome segregation apparatus; **antimitotic agent** = agent that disrupts mitosis.

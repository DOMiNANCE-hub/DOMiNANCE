# CE04 — Analytical Ultracentrifugation and Centrifuge Examples
**Lecturer source:** PDF pp. 366–370. **Editorial status:** source-reconciled; instrument images pending.

## Analytical ultracentrifugation
The lecture calls **analytical ultracentrifugation** a classical biochemistry and molecular-biology method with broad applications. It describes the method as a **primary technique requiring no comparison standards** (p. 366). Sedimentation measurements can investigate solution behaviour across different **solute concentrations and solvents** (p. 367).

The lecture describes it as a further refinement of preparative centrifuges, applicable to small molecules and macromolecules including **ribosomes and proteins** (p. 367). In its specific example, the sample is mixed with **sucrose or caesium chloride medium** in a **quartz centrifuge cell** and spun at forces **up to 500,000 × g**. Sedimentation creates a boundary between clearer solvent above and sample-containing solution below (p. 368).

## Analytical versus preparative
**Preparative** work aims to recover fractions for subsequent use. **Analytical** work follows sedimentation behaviour to characterize molecules. **Understanding note:** analytical ultracentrifugation commonly measures sedimentation velocity/equilibrium optically; it should not be understood simply as a way of obtaining pure samples. The lecture's boundary description illustrates sedimentation rather than guaranteeing complete purification.

## Additional understanding note
A **sedimentation coefficient** may be expressed in Svedberg units (S) and depends on buoyancy and friction as well as mass. This explanation extends the slide text; it is not an explicit statement on pp. 366–370.

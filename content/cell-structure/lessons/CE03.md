# CE03 — Density Gradients, Rate-Zonal and Isopycnic Separation
**Lecturer source:** PDF pp. 353–365. **Editorial status:** source-reconciled; diagrams pending.

## Density-gradient (buoyant-density) centrifugation
The lecturer describes density gradients as a preferred method for purifying subcellular organelles and macromolecules. A medium such as **sucrose** is arranged with **the densest layer at the bottom** and the lightest at the top. Gradients can be **discontinuous or continuous** (p. 353). Two separation categories are **rate-zonal (size)** and **isopycnic (density)** (p. 359).

### Lecturer's sucrose-gradient example (p. 354)
The slides refer to **viruses with densities of 1.1–1.2 g/cm³** in a sucrose gradient. Components with higher buoyant densities band lower in the gradient than less-dense components. **Understanding note:** the slide's printed unit is incomplete in text extraction (g/cm); density is conventionally g/cm³. The phrase that denser particles 'move fastest' is a simplification: movement depends on sedimentation properties and gradient conditions.

## Rate-zonal separation
A sample begins as a zone, usually near the top. Particles separate mainly by **size/mass and sedimentation rate**, rather than equilibrium buoyant density. The lecturer gives **endosomes** and **antibody classes** as examples; antibody classes may have similar densities but different masses (pp. 359–361). **Understanding note:** sedimentation also depends on shape; a run must be stopped before bands all pellet.

## Isopycnic separation
Particles move until the surrounding medium matches their **buoyant density**; at approximate equilibrium, further centrifugation time does not substantially change band position (pp. 362–363). The lecturer's example is **nucleic-acid separation in a CsCl (caesium chloride) gradient** (p. 363). Keep distinct the **rate-zonal size/mass** mechanism and **isopycnic density** mechanism.

## Slide Essentials
- **Continuous/discontinuous sucrose gradients**, dense bottom/light top.
- **Rate-zonal:** size/mass; endosomes; antibody classes.
- **Isopycnic:** equilibrium density; **CsCl nucleic acids**.
- Lecture example: viruses **1.1–1.2 g/cm³** (unit clarified).

## Concept Connections
Differential centrifugation gives partially pure pellets; gradient methods resolve components further. Rate-zonal separates by how fast material travels; isopycnic by where buoyant density matches.

# OR01 — Microbodies and Peroxisomes

**Source:** BIOL 151 Cell Structure, PDF pages 206–209.

**Learning Mission:** Explain the defining structure, major functions, and key distinctions in this lesson.

## Microbodies: the family
**Microbodies** are small, usually oval or spherical, **single-membrane-bound** organelles with specialised metabolic reactions, especially oxidation. The lecture lists **peroxisomes, glyoxysomes, hydrogenosomes and glycosomes** in this group. Microbodies are described in vertebrate liver and kidney, plant leaves and seeds, protozoa, yeasts and other fungi. These organelles are not interchangeable: their enzyme systems and functions differ.

## Peroxisomes
Peroxisomes contain oxidative enzymes. Three named in the slides are **urate oxidase, D-amino acid oxidase and catalase**.

- **Urate oxidase (uricase)** participates in purine breakdown and, in organisms possessing the enzyme, converts urate toward allantoin while generating hydrogen peroxide. **Human nuance:** humans do not have a functional urate oxidase enzyme; the slide describes peroxisomal biochemistry across organisms, not an enzyme active in human liver.
- **D-amino acid oxidase** acts on certain D-amino acids, including those that may originate from gut bacteria, producing corresponding oxoacids and hydrogen peroxide.
- **Catalase** removes potentially harmful hydrogen peroxide: **2 H₂O₂ → 2 H₂O + O₂**.

Peroxisomes also contribute to **fatty-acid metabolism**, amino-acid-related reactions, and detoxification. The slides mention alcohol and formaldehyde as examples of toxic substances undergoing oxidative metabolism. **Understanding note:** this does not mean peroxisomes alone perform all alcohol detoxification; several cellular pathways contribute.

## How peroxisomes increase in number
The lecture notes that peroxisomes can form by division of pre-existing peroxisomes. They can also grow through import of proteins and lipids; modern cell biology recognises contributions from other membrane sources.

## Slide Essentials
Single membrane; oxidative reactions; urate oxidase, D-amino acid oxidase, catalase; H₂O₂ toxicity and removal; fatty-acid metabolism; peroxisome division.

## Concept Connections
**Oxidation** transfers electrons; **H₂O₂** is hydrogen peroxide, a reactive oxidant; **catalase** protects against H₂O₂ accumulation; **oxoacid** is an organic acid with a carbonyl group; **acetyl-CoA** links fatty-acid breakdown with other metabolic pathways.


### Understanding Note — uricase in humans
The lecturer’s list includes **urate oxidase (uricase)** as a peroxisomal enzyme across organisms. **Humans lack functional uricase**; do not infer that human peroxisomes convert uric acid to allantoin through this enzyme.


### Understanding Note — Urate oxidase and human peroxisomes
The lecture lists **urate oxidase** among peroxisomal enzymes (PDF page 207). Preserve that list for lecture-based revision. **Scientific clarification:** humans lack functional urate oxidase (uricase), so human purine catabolism generally ends with uric acid rather than conversion to allantoin by this enzyme. This distinction matters when applying the slide to human physiology.

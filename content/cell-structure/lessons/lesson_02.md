# Lesson 2 — Simple Microscopes

### Types of light microscopes
The two basic types introduced here are **simple microscopes** and **compound microscopes**.

A **simple microscope** uses **one magnifying lens**. Familiar examples are a **magnifying glass** and a **simple single-lens loupe**. A **compound microscope** uses more than one lens system.

### Image formation
A convex (converging) lens can produce an **upright, enlarged virtual image** when the object is placed closer to the lens than its focal point. A *virtual image* appears to be located where light rays seem to come from; the rays do not actually meet at that apparent image position.


### Simple microscopes in use
Modern simple microscopes are often handheld and useful for fieldwork or quick examination of objects. Older designs could be mounted in stands resembling those of more elaborate microscopes, but they still used a single magnifying lens.

A **loupe** is a compact magnifier for examining small details. Unlike a typical handheld magnifying glass, it usually has no attached handle; its lens or lenses may sit in an opaque cylindrical or conical housing or fold into a protective case.

**Understanding note:** Some modern loupes contain multiple lens elements. A loupe is therefore not automatically a *single-lens* microscope simply because it is compact.

### Early contributors
- **Robert Hooke (1635–1703)** — an early figure in microscopy, associated with observations of cork cells.
- **Antony van Leeuwenhoek (1632–1723)** — known for using powerful single-lens microscopes to observe microorganisms.

### Slide Essentials
- **Two types of light microscopes:** simple and compound.
- **Simple microscope:** one lens; examples include magnifying glasses and simple loupes.
- **Modern simple microscopes:** often handheld, suitable for fieldwork and quick viewing.
- **Loupe:** small magnifier, generally without a handle; lens housing may be cylindrical, conical or folding.
- **Hooke:** 1635–1703; **van Leeuwenhoek:** 1632–1723.

### Concept Connections
**Convex lens → magnified virtual image:** A converging lens can enlarge the apparent size of nearby objects. **Simple versus compound microscope:** The distinction concerns the lens arrangement, not merely the instrument's size. **Lens design → image defects:** Even when magnification is achieved, imperfections in lenses may blur or colour the image; these are examined next.

**Optional check:** What distinguishes a simple microscope from a compound microscope? **Answer:** A simple microscope uses one magnifying lens; a compound microscope uses multiple lens systems.

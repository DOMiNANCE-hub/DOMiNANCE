# Cellular Diversity, Size and Shape

**Learning mission:** Relate cellular structure to function and identify factors controlling cell shape.

## 1. Cellular diversity
Cells differ in **size, shape and function**. The lecture states that the human body contains approximately **100 trillion cells** and **200 cell types** (PDF p. 197). These are the slide's approximate teaching figures, not precise modern counts: more recent estimates put total cells in a typical adult in the tens of trillions, with totals varying by body size and counting method. The number of distinguishable cell types also depends on classification.

The key examinable relationship is **form follows function**: cellular shape and internal organization suit the work the cell performs.

## 2. The typical cell
The slide labelled **A typical cell** (p. 198) introduces the general plan of a eukaryotic cell. The broad components are the **plasma membrane**, **cytoplasm** and **nucleus**. These are introduced here; detailed membrane, nucleus and organelle structure belongs in subsequent modules. Refer to the accompanying original slide figure for the specific labels used by the lecturer.

## 3. What determines cell shape?
The lecture (p. 200) lists five influences:

1. **Surface tension of the cytoplasm** — a lecture term for forces influencing cellular form; in modern cell biology, cortical tension at the cell surface is especially relevant.
2. **Viscosity of the cytoplasm** — resistance to internal flow and deformation.
3. **Mechanical action of adjoining cells** — pushing, pulling and contacts with neighbours.
4. **Rigidity of the membrane** — the lecture's wording; membrane-associated cytoskeleton and, where present, the cell wall are major sources of mechanical support.
5. **Functional adaptation** — specialization for the cell's role.

A cell's shape is not determined by a single factor. Its internal scaffolding, environment and function interact.

## Slide Essentials
- Cell size and shape vary with function.
- Lecture estimate: ~100 trillion cells and ~200 types (historical approximations).
- Shape factors: cytoplasmic surface tension, viscosity, adjoining-cell forces, membrane rigidity and functional adaptation.

## Concept Connections
**Shape ↔ function:** specialization changes cellular geometry. **Cytoskeleton ↔ mechanical stability.** **Membrane ↔ boundary and exchange.** **Cell size ↔ surface-area-to-volume ratio** (next lesson).

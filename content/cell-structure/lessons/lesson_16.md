# Lesson 16 — Sectioning and Microtomes

### Sectioning

**Sectioning** is cutting a prepared specimen into thin slices for microscopy. A **microtome** is the instrument used to cut sections of controlled thickness.

The section thicknesses listed are:

- **Light microscopy:** **1,000–20,000 nm** (1–20 µm).
- **Electron microscopy:** **50–100 nm**, with approximately **50 nm** emphasised for ultrathin TEM sections.

### Essential parts of a microtome

All microtomes include:

1. A **specimen holder**.
2. A **sharp cutting edge**.
3. A **mechanism for regulating section thickness**.

**Knife selection:** Steel razor blades are described for wax-embedded specimens; **glass or diamond knives** are used for hard resin-embedded specimens such as Araldite preparations.

**Frozen sections:** A microtome operating inside a freezer at approximately **−20 °C** is used for frozen material. This is commonly called a **cryostat microtome**.

### Mounting sections

- **Light microscopy:** sections are mounted on **glass microscope slides**.
- **Electron microscopy:** ultrathin sections are supported on **fine copper grids**.

### Concept Connections

**Embedding → sectioning:** the support medium prevents delicate tissue from deforming. **Microtome → reproducible thickness:** controlled slicing makes specimens suitable for examination. **Glass slide versus copper grid → different imaging systems:** light microscopy and TEM require different section supports.

### Slide Essentials

**Microtome = specimen holder + sharp cutting edge + thickness regulator.** Steel razor for wax, glass/diamond knife for resin; frozen-section microtome at **−20 °C**; light-microscopy slides versus electron-microscopy copper grids; section thicknesses **1–20 µm** and **50–100 nm**.

**Optional check:** Which cutting edge is suitable for hard resin-embedded material? *A glass or diamond knife.*

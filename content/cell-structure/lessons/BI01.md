# BI01 — Biochemical analysis and spectrophotometry
**Source:** BIOL 151 lecture PDF pp. 371–381. **Status:** editorial review edition; figure and full slide audit outstanding.

## Core lesson

### From fractions to function
Once organelles or cell fractions are isolated, biochemical assays help characterize their activity. The slides identify spectrophotometry, autoradiography and chromatography as example approaches.

### Light absorption
Spectrophotometry measures light transmitted through or absorbed by a sample at selected wavelengths. Absorbance is defined as A = log10(I0/I), where I0 is incident intensity and I is transmitted intensity.

### Beer–Lambert relationship
For suitable dilute solutions, A = εlc, with molar absorptivity ε, path length l and concentration c. Concentration can be estimated from a calibration curve when the assay behaves linearly.

### Limitations and controls
A blank accounts for solvent and reagent background. Scattering, interfering pigments, incorrect wavelength, dirty cuvettes or excessively concentrated samples can distort readings. Colour intensity does not universally reveal the identity of an unknown compound.

### Lecturer-specific spectrophotometer details (PDF pp. 372, 375–376, 380)
The lecturer describes a **spectrometer** that selects a wavelength and a **photometer** that measures the light intensity after it passes through a sample in a **cuvette**. In the described instrument, the photometer's electrical signal goes to a display, illustrated by a **galvanometer**. These are the lecture's instrument terms; contemporary instruments may use electronic digital detectors and displays.

The lecturer's example is **haemoglobin**: it appears red because it absorbs blue and green light more effectively than red light. Under appropriate conditions, the absorption at a selected wavelength can be used to estimate haemoglobin concentration. The slides also mention **water-quality photometers and colorimeters** used to estimate concentrations from colour intensity.

**Understanding note:** The lecturer's descriptions concern colour-dependent absorption. Beer–Lambert calculations are useful additional context, but their equation is not established by the text on these pages and should not replace the lecturer's examinable instrument description.

## Slide Essentials
- Absorbance relates to transmitted light.
- Beer–Lambert A = εlc under suitable conditions.
- Use a blank and calibration.

## Concept Connections
These concepts connect cell preparation, separation and biochemical interpretation: sample preparation affects which components survive, separation enriches material, and subsequent assays determine what is actually present.

# OR04 — Lysosomes in Disease: Silicosis and Tay–Sachs

**Source:** BIOL 151 Cell Structure, PDF pages 219–226.

**Learning Mission:** Explain the defining structure, major functions, and key distinctions in this lesson.

## Silicosis: why lysosomal damage matters
The lecture links **silicosis** to inhalation of silica particles that are engulfed by lung phagocytes. Crystalline silica can damage phagocytes, including lysosomal membranes, provoking inflammation and cell injury. Persistent inflammation promotes **fibroblast activation and collagen deposition**, causing **pulmonary fibrosis**. Scarred lungs are less compliant, so breathing becomes difficult.

The slides compare this broad injury-and-fibrosis pattern with **asbestosis** (asbestos fibres) and **black lung disease/coal workers' pneumoconiosis** (coal mine dust). These are distinct diseases with different exposures and mechanisms; they are not synonyms for silicosis.

## Lysosomal storage disorders
When a lysosomal enzyme is absent or severely deficient, its usual substrate may accumulate within cells. Such diseases are called **lysosomal storage disorders**. The consequences depend on the substrate, the affected tissues and the degree of enzyme deficiency.

## Tay–Sachs disease
The lecture uses **Tay–Sachs disorder** as an example. **HEXA gene** defects cause deficiency of **β-hexosaminidase A**, impairing degradation of **GM2 ganglioside**, a glycolipid especially important in neurons. Accumulation leads to progressive neurological dysfunction.

Classic infantile Tay–Sachs commonly presents with developmental regression, exaggerated startle responses, weakness, later seizures and loss of vision; severe disease can cause early childhood death. The slide mentions Eastern European ancestry; **important clarification:** risk is increased in some populations, including Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry, but the condition can affect people of any ancestry. **Diagnosis** is based on enzyme testing and/or molecular genetic testing; a general chromosome count test is not sufficient.

## Slide Essentials
Silica uptake → phagocyte injury/inflammation → fibroblasts and collagen → reduced lung elasticity; silicosis versus asbestosis versus coal dust disease; enzyme deficiency → lysosomal accumulation; Tay–Sachs → GM2 buildup and neurological decline.

## Concept Connections
**Fibrosis** is excess scar-like connective tissue; **fibroblast** produces extracellular matrix including collagen; **lysosomal storage disorder** involves impaired substrate breakdown; **GM2 ganglioside** is a neuronal membrane glycolipid; **β-hexosaminidase A** is the deficient enzyme in Tay–Sachs.

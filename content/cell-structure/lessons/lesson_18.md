# Lesson 18 — Staining for Electron Microscopy

### Electron contrast rather than colour

Electron micrographs do not use colour dyes to create the primary image. Electron staining increases **electron contrast**: areas that scatter or absorb more electrons appear more **electron-dense** (typically darker in conventional TEM images).

Heavy elements with high atomic numbers are useful because they interact strongly with electrons. Common examples include **lead (Pb)** and **uranium (U)**.

### Heavy-metal staining

- **Uranium compounds:** described as binding preferentially to **nucleic acids and proteins**.
- **Lead compounds:** described as binding to **lipids and membranes**.
- **Osmium:** contributes electron density to **membranes**, as well as other cellular components.

These associations are useful study summaries, not exclusive binding rules: actual staining patterns depend on reagent and specimen preparation.

### Positive and negative staining

**Positive staining** increases the electron density of specimen structures so they appear dark relative to their surroundings. Heavy-metal staining can highlight membranes, organelles and cellular material.

**Negative staining** makes the *background* electron-dense, leaving the specimen relatively light. It is especially useful for small particles such as **viruses, ribosomes and isolated organelle or membrane fragments**.

Negative-stain reagents listed include **potassium phosphotungstate, uranyl acetate and ammonium molybdate**.

### Important comparison

| Light microscopy | Electron microscopy |
|---|---|
| Haematoxylin and eosin (H&E) | Uranium and lead double staining |
| Colour differences make structures visible | Electron-density differences make structures visible |
| Glass slides | Fine copper grids for ultrathin sections |

### Concept Connections

**High atomic number → stronger electron scattering → greater contrast.** **Positive staining → darker specimen structures.** **Negative staining → dark background and light specimen.** **TEM sectioning + staining → transmitted-electron image with useful detail.**

### Slide Essentials

- Heavy-metal contrast agents: **uranium, lead and osmium**.
- Uranium: nucleic acids/proteins; lead: lipids/membranes (slide associations).
- **Positive stain:** specimen darker. **Negative stain:** background darker.
- Negative stains: **potassium phosphotungstate, uranyl acetate, ammonium molybdate**.
- Uranium + lead is compared with **H&E** in light microscopy.

**Optional check:** In negative staining, which area appears electron-dense? *The background around the specimen.*

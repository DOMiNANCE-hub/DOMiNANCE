# Lesson 14 — Fixation

### Meaning and purpose

**Fixation** is the rapid killing and preservation of tissue. It is fundamental to histological preparation because it helps prevent **autolysis** (self-digestion by cellular enzymes), bacterial or fungal attack and damage during subsequent procedures.

Fixation may be **physical** or **chemical**.

### Physical fixation

A specimen can be frozen rapidly by immersion in **liquid nitrogen**, a cryogenic liquid at approximately **−196 °C (−320 °F)**. Rapid freezing aims to minimise formation of damaging ice crystals and preserve tissue structure and enzymatic components.

**Important:** Frozen tissue remains preserved only while kept appropriately frozen. If it warms, autolysis can resume. Permanent preparations of frozen sections may therefore require chemical fixation.

### Chemical fixation

Chemical fixatives stabilise structural components, particularly **proteins and lipids**. Fixatives for electron microscopy should preserve organelles without producing deposits that obscure their structure.

| Light microscopy: listed fixatives | Electron microscopy: listed fixatives |
|---|---|
| Acetic acid, alcohol, acetone, mercuric chloride, chromic acid, formaldehyde | Formaldehyde and glutaraldehyde (protein fixation); osmium tetroxide and potassium permanganate (lipid fixation) |

**Understanding note:** The categories above preserve the slide's examples. In practice, the effects of a fixative depend on its concentration, mixture and protocol; osmium tetroxide is particularly useful for membrane preservation and electron contrast.

### Concept Connections

**Autolysis → fixation:** preservation prevents tissue from digesting itself. **Liquid nitrogen → rapid freezing:** fast cooling reduces ice-crystal damage. **Protein/lipid stabilisation → organelle preservation:** chemical fixation protects the structures that later microscopy aims to reveal.

### Slide Essentials

- Fixation: *rapid killing and preservation of a tissue*.
- Prevents autolysis, microbial attack and damage during processing.
- Physical and chemical methods.
- Liquid nitrogen: **−196 °C**.
- Protein-associated fixatives: **formaldehyde, glutaraldehyde**; lipid-associated: **osmium tetroxide, potassium permanganate**.

**Optional check:** Why might a frozen section deteriorate when allowed to warm? *Freezing preserves it while frozen; autolysis can resume on thawing.*

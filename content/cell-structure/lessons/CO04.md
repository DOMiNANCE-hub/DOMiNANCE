# Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells

**Learning mission:** Distinguish the two major cellular organizations and explain the lecture's listed differences.

## 1. Two fundamental organizations
The lecture introduces **prokaryotes as cells without a nucleus** and **eukaryotes as cells with a nucleus** (PDF p. 190). In eukaryotes, a membrane-bound nucleus encloses most genetic material; prokaryotes lack a membrane-bound nucleus, with DNA generally located in a nucleoid region.

## 2. How the lecture distinguishes prokaryotes
On p. 204, the lecture groups differences into three headings:

**DNA packaging**
- No nucleus.
- DNA described as **not wrapped around histones**. This is broadly appropriate for typical bacteria but **not universal across prokaryotes**: many archaea possess histone proteins or related DNA-packaging proteins. Preserve the lecture's point while remembering this exception.

**Cell wall composition**
- **Bacteria:** where present, cell walls commonly contain **peptidoglycan**.
- **Archaea:** cell walls, where present, use other materials and **lack bacterial peptidoglycan**. Not all bacteria or archaea have a cell wall.

**Internal organization**
- Prokaryotes lack the **complex membrane-bound organelles** characteristic of eukaryotic cells, such as mitochondria and the endoplasmic reticulum. They still have organized internal structures, including ribosomes.

## 3. Comparison table
| Feature | Prokaryotic cells | Eukaryotic cells |
|---|---|---|
| Membrane-bound nucleus | Absent | Present in typical nucleated cells |
| DNA location | Nucleoid region | Mostly nucleus |
| Complex membrane-bound organelles | Absent | Present in many cell types |
| Ribosomes | Present | Present |
| Cell wall | Common in bacteria/archaea; composition varies | Present in plants/fungi, absent in animal cells |

**Clarification:** Mature mammalian red blood cells are eukaryotic-derived cells that lack nuclei. Classification describes cellular lineage and organization, not a claim that every mature eukaryotic-derived cell retains a nucleus.

## 4. Cell theory — assignment support
The lecture lists **Cell Theory** as an assignment (p. 201) but does not present its propositions on these pages. For assignment preparation, standard cell theory states: (1) living organisms are made of one or more cells; (2) the cell is the basic unit of life; (3) cells arise from pre-existing cells. This section is **supporting textbook knowledge**, not a quotation from the slides.

## Slide Essentials
- Prokaryotes: no membrane-bound nucleus; eukaryotes: membrane-bound nucleus in typical cells.
- Three slide comparison headings: **DNA packaging, cell wall makeup and internal structures**.
- Bacterial walls: peptidoglycan where present; archaeal walls: chemically different.
- Prokaryotes lack complex membrane-bound organelles; both groups have ribosomes.

## Concept Connections
**Nucleus → DNA compartmentalization.** **Histones → DNA packaging.** **Peptidoglycan → bacterial cell wall.** **Ribosomes → protein synthesis in both groups.** **Cell theory → continuity of life.**

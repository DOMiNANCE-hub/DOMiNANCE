# CM03 — Membrane lipids, proteins and glycocalyx

**Source:** BIOL 151 PDF pp. 307–317. **Status:** editorial draft, pending image and final academic QA.

## Learning mission
Classify membrane components and explain how proteins support transport, signaling and cell recognition.

## Core lesson
The phospholipid bilayer contains several types of lipids and proteins. The lecture gives **phospholipids as 75%** and **glycolipids as 5%** of membrane lipids in its representative composition. These are slide-specific proportions; membrane composition differs between cell types and organelles.

**Glycolipids** carry carbohydrate groups that face the extracellular side of the plasma membrane. **Glycoproteins** are proteins with attached carbohydrate chains. Together with other surface carbohydrates, they contribute to the **glycocalyx**, a carbohydrate-rich external coat involved in recognition, adhesion and protection.

**Integral membrane proteins** associate strongly with the lipid bilayer; many contain hydrophobic segments buried among lipid tails. **Transmembrane proteins** span the bilayer. **Peripheral proteins** bind more loosely to membrane surfaces or other proteins and can often be detached without disrupting the lipid bilayer.

Membrane proteins perform several functions. **Channels** form selective pathways for particular solutes. **Transporters** bind substrates and undergo conformational changes to move them across. **Receptors** bind signals and initiate responses. Other membrane proteins participate in recognition, attachment, enzymes and anchoring the cytoskeleton. A receptor need not itself transport its ligand.

## Concept Connections
**Integral** describes tight membrane association; **transmembrane** specifies spanning both sides. **Glycocalyx** links surface carbohydrates to recognition. **Channel** provides a pore; **carrier/transporter** binds and changes conformation; **receptor** detects a signal.

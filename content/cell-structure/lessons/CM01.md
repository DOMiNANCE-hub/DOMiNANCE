# CM01 — Cytosol and the phospholipid bilayer

**Source:** BIOL 151 PDF pp. 285–294. **Status:** editorial draft, pending image and final academic QA.

## Learning mission
Distinguish cytoplasm from cytosol, explain why membranes form bilayers, and connect phospholipid structure to membrane function.

## Core lesson
**Cytoplasm** is the cellular region outside the nucleus; it includes cytosol and cytoplasmic organelles. **Cytosol** is its aqueous phase, excluding membrane-enclosed organelles. It contains dissolved ions, simple sugars, proteins and other molecules, and inclusions such as glycogen granules and lipid droplets. Many metabolic reactions occur in the cytosol. The slides describe cytosol as about **55% of cell volume** and **75–90% water**; these are approximate lecture figures, not universal constants.

The **plasma membrane** separates the cell interior from its surroundings. Organelles also have specialized **biomembranes**, whose distinct proteins help them perform different functions. Membranes are not rigid walls: they are dynamic arrangements of lipids and proteins.

A **phospholipid** is **amphipathic**: its polar, hydrophilic head interacts with water while its nonpolar, hydrophobic fatty-acid tails avoid water. In a bilayer, the heads face the aqueous exterior and interior, and the tails face one another. This arrangement creates a selective barrier.

In a common phosphoglyceride, glycerol bears two fatty-acid chains and a phosphate-containing head group. The **fluid mosaic model** describes proteins embedded in or associated with a mobile lipid bilayer. Proteins provide specialized functions; they are not merely decorative parts of the mosaic.

## Slide Essentials
- Cytosol: aqueous cytoplasmic phase; includes dissolved solutes and inclusions (PDF 285–286).
- Plasma membrane: phospholipid bilayer plus associated lipids and proteins (287–290).
- Hydrophilic heads face water; hydrophobic tails form the interior (291–294).
- Amphipathic and fluid mosaic are examinable terms.

## Concept Connections
**Amphipathic** → contains hydrophilic and hydrophobic regions. **Bilayer** → self-assembled arrangement in water. **Selective barrier** → permeability depends on solute properties and transport proteins. **Biomembrane** → shared structural principle, organelle-specific functions.


### Understanding Note — membrane fluidity
Cholesterol generally **restrains excessive phospholipid movement at higher temperatures** while **disrupting tight phospholipid packing at lower temperatures**. Its effect is temperature- and membrane-composition-dependent; avoid describing it as invariably increasing or decreasing fluidity.

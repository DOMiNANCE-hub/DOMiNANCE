# CY01 — Cytoskeleton: architecture and movement

## Learning mission
Explain how a dynamic filament network maintains cell shape, positions organelles and supports transport and movement.

## Core lesson
The **cytoskeleton** is a three-dimensional, dynamic network of protein filaments and associated proteins extending through the cytoplasm. It is not a rigid skeleton: its filaments assemble, disassemble and reorganize as cellular demands change. This enables a cell to maintain a recognizable shape while also changing shape, transporting cargo and sometimes moving as a whole.

Its two broad roles are **structural support** and **transport**. The lecturer likens the filament framework to a **geodesic dome** that balances opposing forces; unlike a rigid dome, cytoskeletal filaments can dismantle in one region and reassemble in another. This allows the cell to change shape while retaining mechanical support. Structural support includes mechanical stability, anchoring and positioning organelles, and organizing specialized cellular regions. Transport includes movement of vesicles and organelles within a cell; cytoskeletal systems also contribute to whole-cell movement. Filaments can be linked to one another or other structures, producing a three-dimensional framework.

The lecturer also lists movement of **cilia, flagella and sperm**, **muscle contraction**, and cytoskeletal participation in **cell division** among its functions. These are distinct examples of cytoskeleton-dependent movement.

**Why red blood cells are not spherical:** the membrane-associated cytoskeleton helps maintain their characteristic flexible biconcave shape. The cytoskeleton is also essential to long, specialized cells such as neurons, which must maintain extended axons while transporting material along them.

The cytoskeleton's organization is constantly remodeled: support and movement are coordinated, not opposing tasks. All three major filament classes contribute to cell architecture, but their mechanical and dynamic properties differ.

## Slide Essentials
- Cytoskeleton: protein-filament network within the cytoplasm, with associated motor and linking proteins.
- Functions: structural support; transport; cell shape change and movement; positioning organelles.
- Highly dynamic, unlike the relatively fixed analogy of a human skeleton.
- Examples: red blood cell shape; long neuronal axons; movement of cellular cargo.
- Lecturer analogy: filaments balance opposing forces like a **geodesic dome**.
- Movement examples: cilia, flagella, sperm, muscle contraction and cell division.

## Concept Connections
**Filament polymerization** = assembling protein subunits into a filament; **depolymerization** = disassembly. **Organelle positioning** links cytoskeletal structure to intracellular specialization. **Membrane skeleton** links filament networks to red-cell shape.

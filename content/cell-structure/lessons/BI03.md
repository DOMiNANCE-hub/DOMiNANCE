# BI03 — Chromatography and partition principles
**Source:** BIOL 151 lecture PDF pp. 388–391. **Status:** editorial review edition; figure and full slide audit outstanding.

## Core lesson

### The two phases
Chromatography separates components through differences in their interactions with a stationary phase and a mobile phase. Components that spend relatively more time with the mobile phase generally migrate faster.

### Partition chromatography
In partition-based separations, substances distribute differently between phases because of their relative solubility. Adsorption and other mechanisms may also contribute in real systems.

### Changing separation
Solvent composition, pH, temperature and stationary phase chemistry can alter retention and resolution. The correct conditions depend on the analytes, not a universal solvent recipe.

### Interpreting movement
In a column example, the less strongly retained substance can elute first. Earlier elution alone does not identify a chemical; standards and controls are needed.

### Lecturer-specific chromatography distinctions (PDF pp. 388–391)
The lecture defines chromatography as separation by distribution between a **stationary phase** and a **moving/mobile phase**. **Partition chromatography** depends mainly on the relative solubility of a substance in two immiscible liquid phases; a solute may dissolve unequally in them. The lecturer contrasts this with **adsorption chromatography**, where attraction to the stationary phase is emphasized, including electrostatic interactions, while noting that adsorption can also occur in partition chromatography.

**Understanding note:** Partitioning and adsorption can coexist in real systems. The lecturer's contrast is a useful introductory distinction, not an absolute rule that all adsorption is purely electrostatic.

## Slide Essentials
- Stationary versus mobile phase.
- Different affinities cause separation.

## Concept Connections
These concepts connect cell preparation, separation and biochemical interpretation: sample preparation affects which components survive, separation enriches material, and subsequent assays determine what is actually present.

# CY02 — Three filament systems and motor proteins

## Learning mission
Distinguish the three major filament classes by composition, approximate diameter and functions, and identify the principal motor-protein families.

## Core lesson
The lecture separates **shapers** (protein filaments) from **movers** (motor proteins). The three filament classes are **microtubules**, **intermediate filaments**, and **actin filaments (microfilaments)**. Each is assembled from protein subunits, but their sizes and functions differ.

| Filament | Lecture diameter | Main protein building blocks | Key roles |
|---|---|---|---|
| Microtubules | ~25 nm outer diameter | α/β-tubulin | Long-distance intracellular transport, spindle organization, cilia and flagella |
| Intermediate filaments | ~10 nm | Tissue-specific proteins such as keratins and neurofilament proteins | Mechanical strength; resistance to stretching |
| Actin filaments | ~8 nm | Actin | Shape changes, cell cortex, contraction, cytokinesis and cell movement |

**Motor proteins** convert chemical energy from ATP into mechanical work. The lecture names **kinesin, dynein and myosin**. Kinesins and dyneins commonly move cargo along microtubules; myosins interact with actin. Specific motors have distinct directions and tasks, so not every kinesin or dynein should be described as doing exactly the same job.

The lecture also distinguishes the roles of the three filament classes: **actin** in membrane contraction, muscle cells and cytokinesis; **intermediate filaments** in resisting mechanical stress; and **microtubules** in cytoplasmic transport, axoneme movement and chromosome movement.

Intermediate filaments are especially important in tissues exposed to mechanical stress. The lecture associates intermediate-filament abnormalities with **epidermolysis bullosa simplex** (keratin defects and skin blistering), **Mallory bodies** in alcoholic liver cirrhosis (keratin-containing inclusions), and **Alzheimer’s disease** in a slide discussing changes in neuronal filaments. Scientific clarification: Alzheimer’s disease cannot be attributed simply to altered neurofilaments; multiple pathological mechanisms are involved, including amyloid and tau-related processes. These are associations, not proof that a single filament change alone explains all cases of a complex disease.

## Slide Essentials
- Three shapers: microtubules, intermediate filaments, actin filaments.
- Three movers: kinesin, dynein, myosin.
- Lecture diameters: 25 nm, 10 nm, 8 nm respectively.
- Keratin filament defects can weaken epidermal cells and cause blistering.
- The lecturer lists **epidermolysis bullosa simplex**, **Alzheimer’s disease**, and **alcoholic liver cirrhosis (Mallory bodies)** under intermediate-filament disorders. This is a lecture classification; the disease mechanisms require the clarification above.
- Mallory bodies contain keratin intermediate filaments; their presence is not exclusive to alcoholic liver disease.

## Concept Connections
**Microfilament** = actin filament. **Motor protein** = ATP-powered protein that generates movement on cytoskeletal tracks. **Mechanical resilience** = ability to withstand physical stress without cell rupture.

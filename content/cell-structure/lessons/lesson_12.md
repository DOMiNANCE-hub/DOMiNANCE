# Lesson 12 — Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) and Microscope Comparison

### Scanning electron microscopy

A **scanning electron microscope (SEM)** uses a beam of electrons to examine the **surface** of a specimen. The electron beam scans across the surface, and detected signals are used to construct an image.

SEM is particularly useful for the surface details of **solid specimens**. The images often have a striking **three-dimensional appearance** because a relatively large depth of the specimen remains in focus.

**Depth of field** means the range of distances that appear acceptably sharp in one image.

Applications include biological surfaces, foreign particles, residues, polymers, electronic components and fractured metal surfaces.

### Features and figures to remember

- Large depth of field compared with ordinary light microscopy.
- High magnification and resolution for detailed surface examination.
- Slides give examples of **up to 100,000× magnification**, and a typical SEM figure of **about 10 nm resolution and 20,000× magnification** unless field-emission SEM (**FESEM**) is used.
- SEM images are **3D-looking**, but a standard SEM image is not automatically a true three-dimensional measurement.

### TEM versus SEM

| Feature | TEM | SEM |
|---|---|---|
| What it mainly examines | Internal ultrastructure of thin specimens | Specimen surface |
| Electron interaction | Electrons pass through specimen | Beam scans surface; emitted/scattered signals detected |
| Image appearance | Fine internal detail, generally 2D projection | Surface detail, often 3D-looking |
| Specimen preparation | Ultrathin sections | Suitable solid specimen preparation |

### Light versus electron microscopes

| Feature | Light microscope | Electron microscope |
|---|---|---|
| Illumination | Visible light | Electron beam |
| Lenses | Glass optics | Electromagnetic lenses |
| Image | Colour possible | Initially monochrome signal/image |
| Resolution | Limited by visible-light wavelength | Can be substantially higher |
| Example maximum magnification in slides | **1,000–1,500×** | **300,000–500,000×** (comparison table) |
| Example resolving power in slides | **~240 nm** | **~0.2 nm** |

The slide comparison gives representative figures, not guaranteed limits of every instrument. **Magnification** enlarges an image; **resolution** determines whether nearby details can be distinguished.

### Slide Essentials
- SEM examines the **surface of solid specimens**.
- SEM offers a **large depth of field** and produces **3D-looking surface images**.
- Slide figures: **100,000×** as an SEM magnification example; **10 nm and 20,000×** for a typical SEM; FESEM may perform better.
- TEM: internal detail through thin sections. SEM: surface topography.
- Light versus electron comparison: **glass versus electromagnetic lenses**, visible light versus electrons, and the slide-specific magnification/resolution figures above.

### Concept Connections
**SEM → surface topography.** **TEM → internal ultrastructure.** **Depth of field → 3D-looking surface images.** **Resolution versus magnification → image detail versus image size.** **Microscope selection → biological question being investigated.**

**Optional check:** Which electron microscope would you choose to examine the surface of a pollen grain? *SEM.*

# Lesson 6 — Resolution and Numerical Aperture

## Resolving power
**Resolution (resolving power)** is the ability to distinguish two closely spaced objects as separate. As objects move closer together, a point is reached where they can no longer be seen as distinct; a microscope with better resolving power can distinguish finer detail.

## Factors that determine resolution

### 1. Numerical aperture (NA)
**Numerical aperture** measures an objective lens's ability to gather image-forming light and resolve fine specimen detail. Fine structures diffract light; collecting a wider cone of these rays helps reveal more detail.

**NA = η sin U**

- **η (eta)** = refractive index of the medium between the specimen and objective lens.
- **U** = half-angle of the cone of light accepted by the objective lens (the slide also uses **u**).
- **Angular aperture** = the angle of the light cone captured by the front of the objective lens.

A wider collected light cone and a higher refractive index increase NA. Careful adjustment of the illumination system helps fill the objective's angular aperture.

For **dry objectives**, air is the medium (refractive index **1.00**). The theoretical NA cannot exceed **1.0** in air; in practice, dry objectives rarely exceed approximately **0.95**.

**Oil immersion** uses a medium whose refractive index is closer to that of glass, reducing loss of high-angle rays and allowing higher NA. The slides give specialized immersion oil at approximately **1.51** and refer to an optimum of approximately **1.55** for glass-covered slides.

**Understanding note:** Refractive index describes how much light slows in a material. A higher-NA objective can collect a wider range of rays, improving detail and often brightness.

### 2. Wavelength
Resolution also depends on **wavelength (λ)**: **shorter wavelengths generally allow finer detail to be resolved**.

### 3. Limit of resolution
The minimum separation that can be distinguished is expressed as:

**r = 0.61 λ / NA**

Here **r** is the limit of resolution, **λ** is wavelength and **NA** is numerical aperture. A **smaller r** means better resolution.

The slides use **NA ≈ 1.4** as a practical high-end value for light-microscope objectives and **green light at λ = 560 nm** as an example. Thus:

**r ≈ (0.61 × 560 nm) / 1.4 = 244 nm ≈ 0.24 µm.**

The slides also give the approximate rule **r ≈ λ/2** and use a typical visible wavelength of about **600 nm**, corresponding to an order-of-magnitude resolution limit near **300 nm**.

**Understanding note:** The exact resolution estimate depends on the optical setup and the formula or approximation used; do not treat every quoted value as identical.

## Slide Essentials
- **Resolution:** distinguishing two nearby objects as separate.
- **NA = η sin U**; η = refractive index; U = half-angle of accepted light cone.
- **Higher NA → generally better resolution** and more collected light.
- **Air refractive index ≈ 1.00**; specialized immersion oil ≈ **1.51**; glass-covered-slide optimum cited ≈ **1.55**.
- **Dry-objective NA:** theoretical maximum **1.0**, practical approximately **0.95**.
- **Shorter wavelength → better resolution**.
- **r = 0.61 λ / NA**; smaller r means finer resolvable detail.
- **High-end objective NA ≈ 1.4**; green-light example **560 nm**.
- **10× objective NA:** plan achromat **0.25**, plan fluorite **0.30**, plan apochromat **0.45**.

## Concept Connections
**Fine detail → diffracted light → angular aperture:** Collecting a wider cone of image-forming light reveals more detail. **Refractive index + angular aperture → NA:** Both determine the light-gathering capacity of an objective. **Higher NA + shorter wavelength → smaller r:** The minimum distinguishable separation decreases. **Oil immersion → increased NA:** An appropriate immersion medium permits high-angle rays to reach the objective. **Aberration correction → usable high NA:** Corrected lens systems help maintain image quality while collecting more light.

**Optional check:** If wavelength stays constant but NA doubles, what happens to r? **Answer:** It halves, so resolution improves.

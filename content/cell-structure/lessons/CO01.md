# The Cell as the Fundamental Unit of Life

**Learning mission:** Define a cell, identify its fundamental properties, and explain why cells are the basis of organismal life.

## 1. What is a cell?
A **cell** is the smallest living unit capable of carrying out the functions of life. The lecture defines it as *the smallest unit of life capable of independently performing life functions* (PDF p. 193). A cell is the **basic living, structural and functional unit of life** (p. 194).

The distinction matters: a tissue contains cells, but the individual cell is the fundamental living unit. Multicellular organisms contain many cells arranged into complex structures; unicellular organisms carry out life functions within a single cell (p. 192).

## 2. Properties of living cells
Cells can **grow, reproduce, process information, respond to stimuli and carry out chemical reactions** (p. 191). These capabilities underpin life. Not every specialized cell reproduces independently: mature human red blood cells, for example, cannot divide. Thus, the lecture's wording describes the general capacity of cellular life, not a universal property of every mature cell.

## 3. Why organismal function depends on cells
The activity of an organism depends on the **individual and collective activities of its cells**. A cell's biochemical activities depend on its **subcellular structures** (p. 194). For example, mitochondria support aerobic ATP production, while the plasma membrane regulates exchange. These examples explain the principle; the organelles are developed in later lessons.

## 4. General functions of cells
The lecture identifies four:

1. **Compartmentalization:** organize chemical reactions within specialized structures.
2. **Regulation of inflow and outflow:** selectively exchange substances with the surroundings.
3. **Use of genetic material:** direct cellular activities through stored biological information.
4. **Continuity of life:** new cells arise from pre-existing cells, transmitting biological information.

## Slide Essentials
- Cell = basic living structural and functional unit of life.
- Unicellular organisms perform life functions in one cell; multicellular organisms coordinate many cells.
- Cells grow, reproduce, process information, respond to stimuli and carry out chemical reactions (general properties).
- The four general functions are compartmentalization, exchange regulation, genetic direction and continuity.

## Concept Connections
**Cell → tissue → organ:** higher levels of organization rely on cells. **Plasma membrane → regulated exchange.** **Genetic material → cellular control.** **Subcellular structure → biochemical function.**

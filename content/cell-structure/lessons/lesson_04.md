# Lesson 4 — Compound and Dissecting Microscopes

## Light microscopy
**Light microscopy** uses visible light to observe specimens. Lens curvature and lens material are selected to reduce distortion of image shapes and colours.

## Two types of light microscopes

**1. Compound microscope.** A modern compound light microscope uses a series of lenses and visible light to magnify a specimen. For transmitted-light viewing, specimens must be thin enough to allow light through them. The instrument typically includes an **eyepiece (ocular lens)**, **objective lenses** (the lenses closest to the specimen), a stage, illumination and focusing controls.

**2. Dissecting microscope.** A dissecting microscope permits viewing of **opaque specimens**—objects that do not allow light to pass through them. It is useful for observing larger specimens and their external features.

**Monocular** means one eyepiece; **binocular** means two eyepieces. These describe the viewing arrangement, not whether the instrument is compound or dissecting.

## Eyepiece or ocular lens
The **eyepiece** is the magnifying lens at the top of a compound microscope that the observer looks through. Common eyepiece magnifications are **10× and 15×**. The **objective lens** is positioned nearest the specimen and provides the first stage of magnification.

**Understanding note:** In a compound microscope, both the objective and eyepiece contribute to the final magnification; their individual powers are not interchangeable.

## Slide Essentials
- **Light microscope:** uses visible light.
- **Compound microscope:** series of lenses; transmitted-light specimens must be thin enough for light to pass through.
- **Dissecting microscope:** allows opaque specimens to be viewed.
- **Eyepiece / ocular lens:** top viewing lens, commonly **10× or 15×**.
- **Monocular:** one eyepiece. **Binocular:** two eyepieces.

## Concept Connections
**Visible light → transmitted illumination → thin specimen:** Light must pass through a specimen for conventional transmitted-light compound microscopy. **Opaque specimen → dissecting microscope:** External surfaces can be examined without transmitted light. **Objective lens + ocular lens → compound magnification:** Both lens systems contribute to image enlargement. **Lens design → aberration control:** Lens composition and curvature influence image quality.

**Optional check:** Which microscope permits opaque specimens to be viewed? **Answer:** A dissecting microscope.

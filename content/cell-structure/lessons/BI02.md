# BI02 — Autoradiography and radioactive tracers
**Source:** BIOL 151 lecture PDF pp. 382–387. **Status:** editorial review edition; figure and full slide audit outstanding.

## Core lesson

### Principle
Autoradiography detects the location of radioactive material using a radiation-sensitive detector. In classical film autoradiography, radioactive emissions sensitize **silver halide crystals** in the photographic emulsion. During photographic development, exposed crystals are converted to **metallic silver**; unsensitized crystals are removed during fixation. The resulting silver grains reveal the radioactive tracer distribution.

### Using labelled precursors
A radioactive precursor can be incorporated into molecules during a biological process. Its subsequent distribution provides evidence about where labelled material has accumulated, but not automatically the identity or activity of every labelled molecule.

### Reading the image
Silver grains or darkened regions correspond to detected emissions, with spatial resolution affected by isotope energy, specimen thickness and exposure. Appropriate controls and radiation-safety procedures are essential.

### Autoradiography versus microscopy
A conventional micrograph shows structural contrast; an autoradiograph maps a tracer signal. They can be correlated but answer different questions.

### Lecturer-specific procedure and application (PDF pp. 381–387)
In classical autoradiography, the labelled specimen is placed in contact with **photographic emulsion**. Radiation sensitizes nearby **silver halide crystals**; photographic development produces **metallic silver grains** that indicate the distribution of radioactive material. The lecture lists **³H (tritium), ¹⁴C, ³²P, ⁴⁵Ca and ³⁵S** and gives **radioactively labelled amino acids** as an example for tracing sites of protein synthesis.

**Scientific clarification:** The lecturer also mentions a **barium meal** in this sequence. A conventional barium meal uses radiopaque barium sulfate for X-ray contrast, **not a radioactive barium isotope** and **not autoradiography**. Preserve the example in lecture review notes, but do not teach the isotope claim as scientifically correct.

## Slide Essentials
- Autoradiography localizes radioactivity.
- Silver halide crystals in the emulsion are converted to metallic silver during development; unexposed crystals are washed away during fixation.
- Silver grains are detection products.
- Signal localization is not proof of molecular identity.

## Concept Connections
These concepts connect cell preparation, separation and biochemical interpretation: sample preparation affects which components survive, separation enriches material, and subsequent assays determine what is actually present.

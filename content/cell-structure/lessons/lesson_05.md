# Lesson 5 — Magnification, Parfocality and Working Distance

## Two functions of microscopy
Microscopes perform two related but distinct functions:
1. **Magnification:** making the specimen appear larger.
2. **Resolution:** making two nearby objects distinguishable as separate objects.

Increasing magnification without improving discernible detail is of little advantage. This is called **empty magnification** when enlargement increases without a corresponding improvement in resolution.

## Magnification
**Magnification** is the ratio of the size of the final image to the actual size of the specimen. It depends on lens focal lengths and their arrangement.

The slide values to remember are:
- Ordinary glass microscopes: approximately **25×–1500×**.
- Some compound microscopes: useful magnification up to **2000×** with an oil-immersion lens.
- Most dissecting microscopes: magnification up to approximately **30×**.
- Above **2000×** for compound light microscopes is generally considered **empty magnification** in this course.

**Understanding note:** These are the course's stated approximate ranges, not universal specifications for every modern microscope.

For a standard compound microscope, **total magnification = eyepiece magnification × objective magnification**. For example, a 10× eyepiece and 40× objective produce 400× total magnification.

## Parfocality
**Parfocal objectives** remain approximately in focus when changing from a lower-power objective (such as 10×) to a higher-power objective (such as 40×). Only minor focusing adjustments should be needed.

## Working distance
**Working distance** is the distance between the front of the objective lens and the specimen. It is relatively long at low magnification and generally becomes shorter as magnification increases.

**Oil-immersion objectives** have a very short working distance and may come extremely close to the specimen. Avoid pressing the lens against the slide or specimen, as this can damage both.

**Understanding note:** On a typical covered slide, immersion oil occupies the gap between the front of the oil-immersion objective lens and the coverslip, rather than requiring the glass lens to touch the specimen.

## Slide Essentials
- **Two functions:** magnification and improved resolution.
- **Magnification:** image-size/specimen-size ratio; depends on focal lengths and lens arrangement.
- **25×–1500×:** ordinary glass microscope range; **2000×:** some compound microscopes with oil immersion.
- **~30×:** most dissecting microscopes.
- **Above 2000×:** generally empty magnification for compound microscopes in the course.
- **Parfocal:** objectives remain approximately in focus after changing magnification.
- **Working distance decreases as magnification increases** (general rule).

## Concept Connections
**Magnification ≠ resolution:** An enlarged blur does not reveal more detail. **Higher magnification → shorter working distance:** The objective comes closer to the specimen. **Parfocal objectives → efficient objective switching:** The image stays nearly focused. **Oil immersion → very short working distance:** Correct handling is essential to protect the lens and slide.

**Optional check:** A 10× ocular and a 40× objective give what total magnification? **Answer:** 400×.

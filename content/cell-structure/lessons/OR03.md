# OR03 — Lysosomes: Structure and Intracellular Digestion

**Source:** BIOL 151 Cell Structure, PDF pages 214–218.

**Learning Mission:** Explain the defining structure, major functions, and key distinctions in this lesson.

## Lysosomal structure
**Lysosomes** are single-membrane vesicular organelles containing **acid hydrolases**—enzymes that digest biological macromolecules. The lecture gives approximately **60 hydrolytic enzymes** and an internal **pH reaching approximately 5.0**. A membrane **H⁺-ATPase** uses ATP to pump H⁺ ions inward until the lumen reaches approximately pH 5.0, maintaining the acidic environment described in the slides.

## Formation and delivery of material
The slides describe **primary lysosomes** arising from Golgi-derived vesicles, then fusing with material-containing compartments to form **secondary lysosomes**. For revision, know this terminology. **Understanding note:** modern cell biology describes a dynamic endosome–lysosome maturation and fusion system rather than only two rigid lysosome classes.

**Receptor-mediated endocytosis** brings extracellular material into **endosomes**. **Phagocytosis** encloses larger particles in **phagosomes**. Fusion with lysosomal compartments enables breakdown of their contents.

## Heterophagy, autophagy and residual bodies
- **Heterophagy:** digestion of material originating outside the cell.
- **Autophagy:** lysosome-dependent breakdown and recycling of the cell's own components, including worn-out organelles such as mitochondria.
- **Residual body:** compartment containing material that has not been completely digested; such material may persist or be expelled.

Digestion products, such as amino acids and sugars, can be transported out of the lysosome for reuse. The lysosomal limiting membrane is protected from the enzymes it contains and helps retain digestive activity within the compartment.

## Autolysis
**Autolysis** refers to breakdown of cells or tissues by their own enzymes, particularly after cell death; it should not be confused with regulated autophagy in living cells.

## Slide Essentials
Golgi/endosomal pathway; acid hydrolases; ~60 enzymes; pH ~5; H⁺-ATPase; primary/secondary lysosome terminology; endosome and phagosome; heterophagy versus autophagy; residual body; autolysis.

## Concept Connections
**Hydrolase** cleaves chemical bonds using water; **endosome** sorts endocytosed material; **phagosome** encloses engulfed particles; **ATPase** hydrolyses ATP to power transport; **autophagy** recycles intracellular components.

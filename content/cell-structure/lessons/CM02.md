# CM02 — Cholesterol and membrane fluidity

**Source:** BIOL 151 PDF pp. 295–306. **Status:** editorial draft, pending image and final academic QA.

## Learning mission
Explain how membrane lipids move and how temperature, fatty-acid saturation and cholesterol affect fluidity.

## Core lesson
Membrane **fluidity** means that lipids and some proteins can move within the plane of the bilayer. **Lateral diffusion** is relatively common. **Transverse movement (flip-flop)** between leaflets is much less common without enzymes that facilitate lipid transfer.

The fatty-acid tails strongly affect packing. **Unsaturated tails** commonly contain cis double bonds that create bends, reducing tight packing and helping maintain fluidity. **Saturated tails** tend to pack more closely. Cooling generally reduces lipid movement and may cause a membrane to shift into a more ordered state; the transition depends on lipid composition.

**Cholesterol** is an important component of animal-cell membranes. At relatively warm temperatures it restrains phospholipid movement; at low temperatures it disrupts close packing and can help prevent abrupt solidification. Thus cholesterol buffers membrane fluidity rather than simply making membranes 'more fluid' in every condition.

The slide illustration contrasts frequent lateral movement with very rare spontaneous flip-flop, giving examples of approximately **10⁷ lateral movements per second** and **once per month** for flip-flop. Treat these as illustrative slide estimates, not fixed rates for every lipid and membrane.

## Slide Essentials
- Lipids and some proteins move laterally (PDF 302).
- Unsaturated tails generally support greater fluidity than saturated tails (303–304).
- Cholesterol restrains movement at warm temperatures but reduces close packing when cool (305).
- Lateral diffusion ≠ transverse flip-flop (306).

## Concept Connections
**Unsaturation** → bends in acyl chains → looser packing. **Temperature** → affects lipid motion. **Cholesterol** → modulates packing. **Flip-flop** → changes membrane leaflet composition and may require enzymes.

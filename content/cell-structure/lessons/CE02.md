# CE02 — Centrifugation and Differential Fractionation
**Lecturer source:** PDF pp. 341–352. **Editorial status:** source-reconciled; diagram approval pending.

## Centrifugation after homogenisation
A **homogenate** is the disrupted cell preparation. The lecture identifies centrifugation as the most widely used method to separate its intracellular components. A centrifugal field accelerates sedimentation; the sedimentation rate depends on **size, density and shape** (p. 342). The lecture names two common techniques for separating bacterial components: **differential centrifugation** and **buoyant-density centrifugation** (p. 341). These methods are also used more broadly in cell biology.

## Differential centrifugation
Repeatedly centrifuge the homogenate at **increasing centrifugal force**. A **pellet** collects at the bottom; the **supernatant** remains above it. Decant or transfer the supernatant and centrifuge it again to obtain successively smaller fractions (pp. 345–348). The lecturer emphasizes that separation is **primarily by particle size**, with preparations that are only **partially pure**. Particle density and shape also affect sedimentation.

### Exact lecture example (p. 349)
1. **1,000 × g for 10 minutes:** unbroken cells and heavy nuclei pellet.
2. **10,000 × g for 20 minutes:** the subsequent supernatant yields a pellet enriched in **mitochondria, lysosomes and microbodies**.

These are the lecture's example conditions, **not universal rotor settings**. Fractions may contain contaminants. The lecturer describes washing pellets by resuspension in **isotonic solvents** and re-pelleting to remove smaller contaminants (p. 350). Differential fractionation is a **preliminary purification step** before density-gradient separation.

## Scientific understanding note
Relative centrifugal force (×g) differs from revolutions per minute (rpm); rotor radius matters when converting between them. This is useful background, not a replacement for the lecture's numeric examples.

## Slide Essentials
- **Differential** and **buoyant-density** centrifugation are the two named methods.
- Preserve the example **1,000 × g/10 min** and **10,000 × g/20 min** and the organelles pelleted.
- Pellet versus supernatant; repeated faster spins; **partial purity**; isotonic washing.

## Concept Connections
Homogenisation releases organelles → differential centrifugation enriches organelle fractions → density gradients further purify them → biochemical markers establish fraction identity.

# CM04 — Selective permeability and electrochemical gradients

**Source:** BIOL 151 PDF pp. 318–321. **Status:** editorial draft, pending image and final academic QA.

## Learning mission
Predict which substances cross membranes readily and explain the combined effects of concentration and voltage gradients.

## Core lesson
A membrane is **selectively permeable**: different substances cross it at different rates. Small, nonpolar molecules such as **oxygen and carbon dioxide** usually cross the lipid bilayer relatively readily. Polar molecules and ions generally require appropriate transport proteins for efficient passage. Water can cross the lipid bilayer to some extent, but **aquaporins** greatly increase water permeability in many cells. Large cargo may be moved by **vesicular transport** rather than diffusing through the bilayer.

A **concentration gradient** is a difference in solute concentration across the membrane. An **electrical gradient** results from a voltage difference. For an ion, these two influences combine into an **electrochemical gradient**. The concentration component favors movement from higher to lower concentration; the electrical component attracts opposite charges and repels like charges. The net direction depends on both, not concentration alone.

The slides use representative animal-cell gradients: **Na⁺** is generally higher outside and **K⁺** higher inside. They also list more O₂ outside and CO₂ inside; these gas patterns are context-dependent and should not be treated as universal across all tissues or conditions. Maintaining ion gradients often requires active transport, including the sodium–potassium pump.

## Slide Essentials
- Nonpolar uncharged molecules such as O₂ and CO₂ can cross the lipid core (PDF 318).
- Specific transmembrane pathways facilitate passage of ions and many polar solutes (318).
- Vesicular transport can move larger cargo (318).
- Concentration gradient + electrical gradient = electrochemical driving forces (319–320).

## Concept Connections
**Selective permeability** determines available routes. **Channels** permit facilitated passage. **Pumps** can build gradients using energy. **Electrochemical gradient** explains ion movement. **Membrane potential** is the electrical component of the driving force.

# BI05 — Chromatograms and retention factor
**Source:** BIOL 151 lecture PDF pp. 402–410. **Status:** lecture TLC diagram and three worked Rf values directly checked; other figures and full slide audit outstanding.

## Core lesson

### Reading a column chromatogram
The slide sequence shows solutes A and B moving down a column. B travels faster and emerges first; A is retained more strongly by the stationary phase. Changing solvent pH or temperature can change elution timing.

### Retention factor in planar chromatography
Rf = distance travelled by the centre of a substance spot from the baseline ÷ distance travelled by the solvent front from the same baseline. Both distances must use the same units and the same starting line.

### Worked example
If the spot moves 3.0 cm and the solvent front moves 6.0 cm, Rf = 3.0/6.0 = 0.50. For a valid conventional run, Rf is usually between 0 and 1.

### What Rf can tell you
Rf depends on stationary phase, solvent, temperature and other conditions. Comparing Rf values is useful only under matching conditions, and a matching Rf alone does not prove two samples are identical.

### Lecturer-specific chromatogram and Rf interpretation (PDF pp. 402–410)
In the illustrated **column chromatogram**, solvent carries solutes **A and B** down a column; **B moves faster and emerges first**, whereas A is more strongly retained. The lecture notes that changing **solvent pH or temperature** can change separation and elution.

For paper chromatography or TLC, **Rf = distance from origin to centre of the solute spot ÷ distance from origin to solvent front**. Both distances must be measured in the same direction and units. The lecturer calls Rf the **retention factor** on p. 398 and **“ratio of fronts”** on p. 404. Keep both lecture terms in the internal source record; use *retention factor* as the standard teaching term. Rf is useful for comparison **under identical experimental conditions**, not as a universally unique fingerprint.

**Verified lecture worked TLC example (PDF pp. 407–410):** The solvent front is **7.7 cm** from the origin. Spot 1 moves **1.4 cm**, so Rf = **1.4/7.7 = 0.18**; spot 2 moves **6.0 cm**, so Rf = **6.0/7.7 = 0.78**; spot 3 moves **4.1 cm**, so Rf = **4.1/7.7 = 0.53**. These are the values printed on the lecturer’s worked solution (PDF p. 410). Read the diagram and formula together: the numerator is always the distance to the **centre** of the substance spot from the same origin.

## Slide Essentials
- Rf = spot distance / solvent-front distance.
- Column elution order reflects retention.
- Rf comparison requires matched conditions.

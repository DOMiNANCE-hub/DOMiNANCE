## Predict packing under stated conditions
Hold temperature and other composition constant while comparing cis-unsaturated with saturated tails. A cis bend disrupts close packing; straighter saturated chains can associate more closely. This explains the source's packing figure, rather than merely supplying the slogan “unsaturated is fluid”. Actual PDF pp.303–304 were visually checked for this comparison.

| Condition | Cholesterol's useful comparison |
|---|---|
| Relatively warm membrane | Restrains excessive phospholipid movement |
| Cooling membrane | Interferes with close packing/abrupt solidification |

Those effects are compatible: buffering depends on the starting condition. “Cholesterol always increases fluidity” and “cholesterol always stops movement” both lose the conditional reasoning. Lateral diffusion occurs within a leaflet; flip-flop requires a polar head to cross the unfavourable nonpolar core, so spontaneous transfer is comparatively rare.
**Optional check:** Can two membranes at the same temperature have different fluidity? **Answer:** Yes. Tail saturation, cholesterol and other composition differences affect packing and movement; temperature alone is not enough.

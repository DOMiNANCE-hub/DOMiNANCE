## Use a common origin and consistent units
In an original exercise, the baseline is 1 cm above the ruler's zero, the spot centre reads 3 cm and the solvent front reads 6 cm. The travel distances are 2 cm and 5 cm, so Rf=2/5=0.40. Using raw ruler readings would give 3/6=0.50, answering the wrong geometry.
An Rf is dimensionless because numerator and denominator use the same distance unit. If a spot's travel distance is 20 mm and the solvent front's is 5 cm, first convert 20 mm to 2 cm. A value outside the conventional 0–1 interval calls for checking the origin, front, units and measurement, not announcing a uniquely identified chemical.
Actual PDF pp.407/410 were visually inspected: the printed worked solution uses 1.4/7.7≈0.18, 6.0/7.7≈0.78 and 4.1/7.7≈0.53. Those are printed-source values, not newly claimed pixel-precise measurements of the illustration.
**Optional check:** Why compare Rf only under matched conditions? **Answer:** Solvent, stationary phase and experimental conditions affect retention; matching one ratio does not independently prove chemical identity.

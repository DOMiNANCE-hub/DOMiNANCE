## Compare two causal chains
In the storage-disorder example, reduced β-hexosaminidase A activity impairs a particular degradation step, so GM2 substrate accumulates and cell function is affected. The causal link is a missing or deficient reaction, not simply that a cell contains “too many lysosomes”. A general chromosome count does not measure this enzyme activity or identify a HEXA sequence variant.
In the silica example, particle uptake can injure phagocytes and lysosomal membranes, contributing to inflammation and fibrosis. Fibrosis changes lung mechanics through tissue injury and collagen deposition; it is not the same mechanism as failure to degrade GM2. Similar organelle involvement does not make silicosis and Tay–Sachs synonyms.
These are educational mechanisms, not diagnostic advice; the original qualifications about ancestry, phenotype and testing remain necessary.
**Optional check:** Why can a specific lysosomal enzyme defect affect one substrate strongly without abolishing all intracellular digestion? **Answer:** Different enzymes catalyse different steps, so a specific blocked reaction can cause selective substrate accumulation.

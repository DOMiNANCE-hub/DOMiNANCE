## Compare organising centres with the structures they organise
Use three levels of organisation. A tubulin dimer is a molecular building unit. A microtubule is a hollow polymer. A centrosome is a larger organising region containing pericentriolar material and, typically in animal cells, a pair of centrioles. The pericentriolar material nucleates microtubules; a centriole is not simply one microtubule enlarged.

A typical centriole has nine peripheral triplets. A basal body retains that triplet organisation while helping organise a cilium. Many motile ciliary shafts instead have nine peripheral doublets around two central singlet microtubules: 9+2. The central pair are two separate microtubules, not a tenth doublet. Not all cilia follow that motile pattern; many primary cilia have a 9+0 axoneme and sensory functions.

In a typical radial centrosomal array, minus ends lie near the organising region and dynamic plus ends extend toward the cell edge. During division the system is reorganised into a spindle. Centrosome, centriole, basal body and axoneme therefore describe related but distinct structures and should not be substituted for one another in an explanation.

**Optional check:** Does nine triplets around a basal body mean its ciliary shaft must also contain nine triplets? **Answer:** No. Basal-body triplets and axonemal doublets describe different regions; many motile shafts have nine doublets and two central singlets.

## Choose a filament from the task
Mechanical resilience, motor-driven transport and cell-edge contraction are related but distinct functions. Intermediate filaments resist deformation; actin supports the cortex and myosin-associated movement; microtubules provide many long-range tracks and organise the spindle. A filament is not itself identical to a motor protein.
The lecture's approximate diameters are useful distinctions: 25 nm, 10 nm and 8 nm for the respective classes. They are representative structural dimensions, not a rule that a routine light microscope can resolve and identify every individual filament by width.
The disease associations require their existing caveats. Keratin-related blistering illustrates loss of mechanical support, while Alzheimer-related tau pathology cannot be reduced to one universal intermediate-filament defect.
**Optional check:** A vesicle needs directed movement along a microtubule. Is naming tubulin alone enough to explain the movement? **Answer:** No. Tubulin forms the track; motor proteins and their energy-dependent interactions provide directed transport.

## Read the polymer at two different scales
An α/β-tubulin heterodimer repeats head-to-tail along a protofilament. Typically 13 protofilaments associate side-by-side around a cytoplasmic microtubule's hollow wall. Thirteen therefore counts longitudinal chains across the circumference, not dimers in the whole length. The outer diameter of approximately 25 nm is a separate measurement.

[Figure:CELL-MT-POLARITY]

The repeated orientation makes the ends structurally different. It can help organise motor traffic but does not assign opposite electrical charges. Plus ends often grow or shrink more rapidly in the radial array taught here; plus does not mean permanent growth, nor does minus mean permanent shrinkage. Dynamic instability concerns switching of growth and shrinkage, while directional cargo movement requires motors and energy. A motor carrying cargo and a tubulin dimer joining the polymer are different events.

**Optional check:** Does a microtubule with 13 protofilaments contain only 13 tubulin dimers? **Answer:** No. Each protofilament contains many dimers along its length; 13 describes the usual number of parallel chains in the wall.

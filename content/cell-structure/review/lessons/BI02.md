## Separate a tracer observation from an identity claim
Radiolabel incorporation, radioactive emission, emulsion exposure and photographic development are different stages. Exposed silver halide becomes metallic silver during development; the visible grain is detector evidence, not a cell organelle turning into silver.
A labelled precursor's distribution can indicate where label accumulated, but metabolism and experimental timing determine what it now represents. Background controls and complementary evidence prevent assuming that every grain proves a particular protein's identity or activity.
Keep the existing barium-meal correction: radiopaque X-ray contrast and a radioactive tracer are different signal mechanisms. A similar-looking clinical image does not make them interchangeable.
**Optional check:** Why does a dark grain not automatically identify the exact labelled molecule? **Answer:** It reports detected radioactivity; assigning the signal to a molecule requires knowledge of the precursor, processing, controls and possible redistribution.

## Diagnose a failed chromatogram
The baseline must begin above the solvent surface so the applied sample enters the developing separation through the paper rather than dissolving directly into the reservoir. Capillary movement carries the mobile phase upward; different interactions create different migration distances.
Mark the solvent front promptly because evaporation can remove the visible reference. Without that common reference, an Rf calculation cannot be reconstructed reliably from a dry strip merely by measuring the highest spot.
If two components overlap in one solvent, a second development direction and different solvent can provide another separation condition. Identical movement in one system alone is not conclusive identity. These are interpretation principles rather than a validated chemical-handling protocol.
**Optional check:** A sample spot started below the solvent level. Why is a missing spot not proof that no sample was present? **Answer:** It may have washed into the reservoir instead of undergoing the intended paper separation.

### Worked distinction: bigger, separated or visible?
Imagine two tiny marks only 0.15 µm apart. An optical system whose minimum resolvable separation is 0.24 µm cannot distinguish them as separate under those assumed conditions. Enlarging the recorded blur makes it bigger, not resolved. Increasing contrast may make the blur easier to detect without separating the two marks.

| Change | Direct purpose | What it cannot guarantee |
|---|---|---|
| Enlarge an image | Magnification | New fine detail |
| Distinguish neighbouring points | Resolution | Strong contrast |
| Make specimen differ from background | Contrast | Smaller diffraction limit |

These are related requirements rather than interchangeable quality words. Convert the units first: 0.15 µm=150 nm and 0.24 µm=240 nm. Comparing 0.15 directly with 240 without reconciling units would reverse the apparent meaning.
**Optional check:** If two marks remain merged after digital enlargement, which capability has enlargement failed to add? **Answer:** Resolution; making the same blur larger has not separated the two points.

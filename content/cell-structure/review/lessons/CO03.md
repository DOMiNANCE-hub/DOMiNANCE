## Worked extension: division increases exchange area at fixed total volume
One cube of side 2 cm has volume 8 cm³ and surface area 24 cm². Divide that volume into eight separate cubes, each of side 1 cm: total volume remains 8×1=8 cm³, but exposed surface becomes 8×6=48 cm² if every face is accessible. Total surface doubles, while total volume does not change. The smaller units each have SA/V=6 cm⁻¹, compared with 3 cm⁻¹ for the original cube.

[Figure:CELL-SA-V]

This is a geometric model, not a claim that cells are centimetre-sized cubes or that dividing tissue exposes every cell face to the outside. In tissue, neighbouring surfaces may contact each other and transport pathways matter. For a sphere, the corresponding expression is SA/V=3/r, so the inverse-length relationship also appears in another shape. Changing shape or adding folds can increase surface without the same proportional increase in volume.

**Optional check:** Does a larger cube have less total surface, or less surface per unit volume? **Answer:** Less surface per unit volume. Its absolute surface area increases, but volume increases faster.

## Distinguish a drug's use from its action on a dynamic system
Both vincristine and paclitaxel are anticancer drugs. The discriminating source-backed comparison is their action on microtubule dynamics: the lecturer groups vincristine with inhibitors of assembly, while Taxol/paclitaxel stabilises microtubules and inhibits disassembly. The original broad question asking which one is used in chemotherapy could therefore admit two options. The protected review question now specifies inhibition of assembly and retains the original B position for vincristine.

| Perturbation | Why spindle function can fail | What must not be inferred |
|---|---|---|
| Suppressed assembly | Necessary spindle tracks cannot form or remodel normally | Only tumour cells contain tubulin |
| Suppressed disassembly | Existing tracks cannot undergo normal rearrangement | Polymer presence guarantees successful division |
| Disruption of motor-track coordination | Force and cargo/chromosome movement can be impaired | Every structural filament is itself a motor |

Normal cells use microtubules for division and intracellular transport too. A useful anticancer effect is therefore not proof of complete tumour selectivity or absence of adverse effects. This lesson explains a cellular mechanism; it does not supply dosing or prescribing recommendations. Colchicine and nocodazole are mechanistic examples in the lecture and must not all be described as interchangeable routine cancer treatments.

**Optional check:** If both paclitaxel and vincristine can disrupt mitosis, what feature distinguishes them in this lesson? **Answer:** Their contrasting effects on microtubule assembly and disassembly, rather than whether only one has anticancer use.

### Trace the light that reaches the objective
In bright-field, direct transmitted light contributes to a bright background; absorption or refraction by the specimen produces differences against it. In dark-field, the central direct rays are excluded from the objective, but specimen-scattered rays can enter it. The contrast reverses because the detected light takes a different path, not because the specimen necessarily emits light.
An unstained microorganism can therefore be conspicuous in dark-field while remaining hard to distinguish in ordinary bright-field. Fluorescence is a different mechanism: an excited label emits detectable light. Neither dark-field brightness nor a black background alone establishes that a fluorescent antibody was used.
**Optional check:** An unstained particle is bright against a dark background because it scatters illumination into the objective. Is fluorescence required? **Answer:** No. This is the dark-field mechanism; scattering is distinct from fluorescent emission.

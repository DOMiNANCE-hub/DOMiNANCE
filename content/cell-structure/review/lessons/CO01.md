## From cell architecture to biological function
The lecturer's general-function list describes how cellular life is organised, rather than a checklist that every differentiated cell performs independently. A unicellular organism can regulate exchange, use genetic information, obtain energy and reproduce within one cell. A specialised cell in a multicellular organism may depend on neighbouring cells for supplies, signalling and survival. Mature mammalian red blood cells provide an important exception to a literal universal statement about nuclear genetic direction: they have lost their nucleus and cannot divide.

Compartmentalisation separates reactions and creates useful local conditions. A lysosome's acidic lumen favours its degradative enzymes; a membrane barrier and regulated transport help maintain the difference from the surrounding cytosol. Structure therefore affects function through enzyme location, substrate access and local chemistry. Chemical ingredients alone do not explain where or when a reaction occurs. The plasma membrane is both a boundary and an exchange interface, rather than a completely sealed bag.

**Optional check:** Does a specialised cell's inability to reproduce independently disprove cell theory? **Answer:** No. Cell theory identifies the basic units of organisms; differentiated cells can depend on a multicellular system and lose reproductive capacity.

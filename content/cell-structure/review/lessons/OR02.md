## Follow the carbon rather than confuse similar organelle names
In an oil-rich germinating seed, stored lipid supplies carbon before photosynthesis is fully established. Fatty-acid breakdown supplies acetyl-CoA. The glyoxylate-cycle bypass conserves carbon that would otherwise be lost through the carbon-dioxide-releasing steps of the citric-acid cycle, allowing linked pathways to contribute carbon to carbohydrate synthesis. The source names succinate as a net product; additional mitochondrial and other metabolic steps are needed before gluconeogenic sugar formation. Succinate is not simply converted directly into glucose in one reaction.

| Name | Defining teaching distinction | Misinterpretation to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Glyoxysome | Specialised plant microbody supporting lipid-carbon use for sugars | A human organelle that directly changes fat into glucose |
| Hydrogenosome | Certain anaerobic eukaryotic energy pathways release H₂ | A compartment that releases H₂O₂ because of its name |
| Glycosome | Listed in the source; supplementary account concerns compartmentalised glycolysis in some protozoa | A synonym for a glycogen storage granule |

Human metabolism should not inherit the seed pathway merely because both cells contain peroxisomes. The role of a compartment depends on its organism and enzymes. The detailed glycosomal pathway remains outside what this source passage actually teaches.

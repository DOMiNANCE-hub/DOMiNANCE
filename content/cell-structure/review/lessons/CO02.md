## Explain a shape change rather than memorising a list
Separate a cell's functional adaptation from the forces acting on it. A long neuronal process supports communication over distance. A red cell's flexible biconcave geometry supports gas exchange and passage through narrow vessels. These applications explain why shape can suit a task; they do not imply that every cell actively selects its outline in response to a need.

For a mechanical example, imagine neighbouring cells pressing on one cell while its other properties remain similar. Its outline may change because contact forces are transmitted through the membrane and underlying cortex. Membrane-cortex stiffness influences the response; cytoplasmic viscosity affects resistance to flow. The lecturer's term membrane rigidity should not be interpreted as a perfectly rigid wall. Membrane, cytoskeleton, internal pressure and external attachments jointly contribute to mechanics.

Distinguish three kinds of statement: a measured dimension for a particular cell, an approximate population estimate, and a causal explanation. The lecturer's historical 100-trillion total is an estimate, not a dimension or an exact count applicable to every person. Neither cell number nor the colour of a diagram supplies a mechanism for a shape change.

**Optional check:** A cell flattens when compressed by its neighbours. Which source-listed influence is most directly illustrated? **Answer:** Mechanical action of adjoining cells; the cell's own membrane-cortex properties affect how it responds.

## Build a ribosomal subunit in the correct sequence
Nucleolar organisation brings rRNA genes, growing RNA and assembly proteins together without enclosing them in a lipid membrane. Nucleolar organiser regions contain rRNA-gene arrays; they are not simply another name for centromeres or all chromosome ends. Transcription and RNA processing supply major rRNA components, while ribosomal proteins made on cytoplasmic ribosomes are imported for assembly. Pre-ribosomal subunits are exported through nuclear pores and mature further before normal translation.

This sequence explains why a visible nucleolus is associated with ribosome biogenesis rather than being the place where all cellular proteins are made. It also explains why a cell with high protein-production demands may have prominent nucleolar activity. Number and appearance depend on cell state, active gene regions and organisation; the slide's fixed-number-per-species statement should not be applied as a universal counting rule. Nucleoli reorganise during division.

**Optional check:** A newly made ribosomal protein is detected first in cytoplasm and later in a nucleolus. Is that inconsistent with nucleolar ribosome assembly? **Answer:** No. Ribosomal proteins are translated in cytoplasm and imported to assemble with rRNA; synthesis of a component and assembly of the subunit occur in different places.

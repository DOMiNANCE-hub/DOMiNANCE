## Rate and destination answer different questions
In rate-zonal separation, compare how far particles move in a limited time. Different sedimentation rates create separated zones, which are lost if everything eventually pellets. Similar densities do not prevent a rate difference caused by size or shape.
In isopycnic separation, compare the equilibrium density at which a particle bands. Particles of the same buoyant density may share a band despite different sizes, while particles of different densities can reach different positions. More time after equilibrium does not turn band position into a direct size measurement.
For a fictional gradient spanning 1.05–1.25 g/cm³, a particle of buoyant density 1.15 g/cm³ should band where the medium matches 1.15, assuming an appropriate gradient and equilibrium conditions. This is an original interpretation example, not a new lecturer-specified protocol.
**Optional check:** Two proteins have equal buoyant density but different sedimentation rates. Which distinction matters when choosing a method? **Answer:** Rate-zonal can exploit a rate difference; isopycnic equilibrium chiefly resolves density rather than that rate difference.

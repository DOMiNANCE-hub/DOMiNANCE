## Follow both driving forces for an ion
Suppose a positive ion is more concentrated outside a cell, while the interior is electrically negative. Its concentration tendency is inward, and its electrical tendency is also inward. If the voltage tendency opposed the concentration tendency, direction could not be decided from concentration alone. The combined electrochemical gradient matters.
This reasoning describes a driving force, not proof that a route exists: an impermeable lipid core may require a suitable channel or transporter before substantial movement occurs. Oxygen is relatively compatible with that core; a charged ion encounters a different chemical barrier. Water permeability can be increased by aquaporins without turning them into pumps for every solute.
**Optional check:** Does a concentration gradient guarantee that every substance crosses the membrane rapidly? **Answer:** No. Permeability and the available transport pathway also determine whether movement can occur efficiently.
